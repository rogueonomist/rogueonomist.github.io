3.2 New This Week — Single-Instrument Diversification Flag (Sec. 3.2(d))
ImpactIPS 3.2(d) caps a single instrument at 20% of total fixed-income allocation excluding sovereign instruments and bank placements. That ex-sov/bank sleeve is COI + the three STS lines = PKR 1,977mn. COI alone is PKR 1,329mn of that sleeve — 67.2%, well past the 20% test — even though it separately clears the DFI-specific 15% AAA cap (Sec. 3.3(b)) at portfolio level.
ActionThis reads as a genuine tension between two IPS clauses (the DFI sub-limit vs. the general instrument-diversification test) rather than a clean breach. Needs an interpretive call from the Board/IC on whether 3.2(d) was intended to bind DFI placements — which already carry their own 3.3(b) cap — or only corporate bonds/Sukuk/CP. Flagged, not softened, either way.

Read-throughA 60% hold probability at the 27-Jul meeting, against a curve that's pricing in "stable," supports treating the COI/Pak Oman rollover (Lever 1, Sec. 6) as a repricing decision on today's levels rather than a rate call to time around. Methodology footnotes (v1): (i) r̄ estimated as the trailing mean ex-post real rate over the 2019–2026 CPI sample in hand (81 monthly obs) — no cross-country proxy substitution used, per instruction; std. dev. of 7.3pp across the sample reflects genuine regime swings (COVID near-zero real rates through the 2023 22% tightening peak), not estimation noise, and will be refit as CPI history lengthens. (ii) BVAR risk premium/CDS and Current Account terms are excluded — not present in project files; FX reserves MoM% substitutes for the external-sector term. (iii) Probit trained on 47 MPC meetings (2020–2026); in-sample direction hit rate 55% vs. a 47% naive "always hold" baseline — a genuine but modest edge, not yet validated out-of-sample given the small n. (iv) Policy rate history 2019–2026 reconstructed from SBP's published rate-change archive cross-checked against contemporaneous press reporting; ~60% of meeting-level rates are directly confirmed, the remainder bracketed with medium confidence — flagged individually in the underlying data file and due for one manual verification pass against SBP's source table.

Scenario +100 / +50 bps — Rate Shock Up
ImpactWeekly Alpha would compress toward −59bp (+100bp) or −9bp (+50bp) on a mechanical, same-day basis — the sharpest downside case for the headline KPI, purely because the floating benchmark re-prices faster than the fixed book. MTM loss is immaterial (≤0.27% of portfolio) given the ~99-day duration. The Pak Oman facility cost rises PKR 1.5–3.1mn/quarter, widening COI's already-negative carry (Sec. 3.3) further.
Trade RecommendationDo not extend duration or lock new 1Y/PIB paper into a rising curve. Hold the <3M T-Bill bucket at or above its current 39.4% weighting so maturities roll into the higher curve quickly — this is what converts the −59bp mechanical alpha hit into the +95bp reinvestment pickup shown above. Treat Lever 1 (COI/Pak Oman rollover, Sec. 6) as more urgent, not less: a confirmed +50–100bp facility reset before 31-Jul makes the negative-carry problem materially worse if left undecided.
Scenario −50 / −100 bps — Rate Cut (Rally)
ImpactWeekly Alpha would appear to widen toward +91bp (−50bp) or +141bp (−100bp) — but this is the mirror-image mechanical effect (fixed book, falling benchmark) and is not durable. The real economic exposure is reinvestment giveaway: −55bp to −105bp versus the current 11.29% book average on every rupee that rolls over. With 39.4% of the book maturing within 3 months and a further 37.1% within 12 months (Sec. 3.1), that giveaway compounds quickly across roughly three-quarters of the portfolio.
Trade RecommendationLock in duration ahead of the move rather than after it. The 2Y–3Y segment is already the richest point on the curve at 11.29–11.32% PKRV (Sec. 1) — consistent with the 33–34% into 3Y PIB sizing already modeled to reach the board's ~1.0-year duration ceiling in the 8-Jul duration-extension analysis. In a −100bp scenario this becomes the highest-value action available: extending now captures ~140bp more running yield per rupee than waiting for the cut to land. Treat the 31-Jul COI rollover as a "lock now" opportunity — re-pricing before a confirmed cut lands more yield than re-pricing after.
