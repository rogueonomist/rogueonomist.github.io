Marginal Price Volatility
Primary barrier to CTBCM project finance

ISMO settles every hourly imbalance at the system marginal price — the variable-cost of the last generator dispatched. Across 27 months of actual data, this price ranges from Rs 10.0 to Rs 50.24/kWh, with a coefficient of variation of 36.3%. Lenders cannot model this as a fixed cost.

Impact
A 10 MW BPC faces Rs 84–215M/year net imbalance cost that accrues hourly and is entirely unhedged. Project finance debt sizing requires cash buffers that make bilateral projects unviable.
NCGCL Response
Module A price collar caps and floors the settlement price on imbalance MWh. CTBCM Imbalance Guarantee (CIG) provides excess-of-loss cover above retention floor.
High
Solar Duck Curve: Structural Mismatch
Emerged March 2026 — permanent shift

Before March 2026: daytime MC > nighttime MC (industrial load aligned with gas/RLNG peaks). From March 2026: solar collapses midday MC while evenings spike. April 2026: H14 = Rs 23.91/kWh vs H20 = Rs 50.24/kWh — an 83% intraday swing. Solar CS portfolios now face systematic mismatch: overproducing when MC is cheapest.

Impact
Solar CS generates peak output at H10–H14 (lowest MC) while BPC demand peaks at H18–H22 (highest MC). Every sunny day creates structural deficit imbalance at expensive hours.
NCGCL Response
Fund pricing matrix applies technology surcharge to solar-heavy CS portfolios during Mar–Sep. BESS requirement in Module B reduces intraday mismatch by ~62%.
High
Non-Compensable Curtailment
Grid curtailment with no settlement remedy

When NTDC/DISCO issues a Must-Stop instruction, the CS generator goes offline — but the BPC still consumes. ISMO settles the deficit at the prevailing MP with zero compensation to the CS. At April 2026 evening prices (Rs 46–50/kWh), each curtailed MWh costs Rs 46,000–50,000 in uncompensated settlement exposure.

Impact
CS has no contractual recourse for Must-Stop curtailments under MCC. Grid infrastructure constraints (particularly in corridor-concentrated corridors) make this a recurring, unpriced risk.
NCGCL Response
Module B curtailment rider covers verified non-compensable curtailment exposure from Hour 2 onward (Hour 1 absorbed by BESS deductible). ISMO curtailment reason-code is verification anchor.
Med
Benchmark Calibration: Fund Not Self-Sustaining
Critical design flaw in ISMO Annex-7 draft

The ISMO draft sets benchmark at "50% of monthly reference industrial grid price" — implying ~Rs 8.5/kWh if tied to the tariff's fuel cost component. At this level, only 14.7% of months fall within the 75/125 band. The Fund pays out in 85%+ of months with zero CS-to-Fund receipts. This is not a collar — it is a one-way subsidy instrument.

Impact
Premium income cannot offset expected losses at ISMO draft parameters. At Rs 8.5 benchmark with 75/125 band, Fund capital depletes within 6–12 months. Product is commercially unviable at launch.
NCGCL Response
Recalibrate benchmark to trailing 3-month average ISMO MP (Rs 12–18 range). Adopt 50/150 band width. Raises in-band coverage to 57–72%, enabling genuine two-way risk transfer.
Med
DISCO / UoSC Payment Risk
Systemic settlement disruption

Use-of-System Charges (Rs 6–9/kWh) are owed by BPCs to DISCOs for wheeling. DISCO payment delays — a structural feature of circular-debt dynamics — disrupt CS cash flows even when bilateral contracts are performing. ISMO identified this as the most significant structural barrier to CTBCM scalability.

Impact
CS may be technically solvent on bilateral contract but face liquidity stress from DISCO payment delays. Commercial banks cannot distinguish this from credit default, inflating borrowing costs.
NCGCL Response
UoSC/DISCO Payment Backstop Facility (UPAF): 30-day payment guarantee (Phase 1 fast track) plus structured dual-layer backstop (Phase 2). Not sovereign guarantee — transitional liquidity bridge.
Med
Lender Bankability Gap
Project finance cannot underwrite market risk

Commercial banks and project-finance lenders have no actuarial framework to price CTBCM imbalance risk. The result: lenders impose cash buffers of 8–15% of contract value as liquidity reserves — directly reducing debt capacity and project viability. Without a rated guarantor converting this risk into a defined, capped exposure, CTBCM will remain undersubscribed.

Impact
Estimated Rs 44–96M/year saving from CTBCM (10 MW consumer) is negated by Rs 42–120M in mandatory cash buffers demanded by lenders. Net benefit approaches zero on leveraged projects.
NCGCL Response
CTBCM Project Finance Guarantee (CPFG): second-loss portfolio guarantee to FIs at 1.3× DSCR floor. Transforms unratable imbalance risk into a NCGCL AAA-rated defined guarantee. Bankability restored.
