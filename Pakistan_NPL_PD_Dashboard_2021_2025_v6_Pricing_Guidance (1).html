Conditional Guarantee Pricing and Expected Credit Loss
How to use the Indicative Pricing & ECL tab

This tab starts with an institution-level probability of default and converts it into the expected and unexpected cost of a specific guarantee structure. Read the model from left to right: PD → correlated portfolio defaults → portfolio loss → legal guarantee waterfall → ECL and economic capital → annual guarantee fee. Changing a structure does not change the institution PD; it changes which portion of portfolio loss NCGCL absorbs.

1Select the risk anchor

Choose the bank or MFB and the PD basis. For MFBs, compare reported PD with the COVID-adjusted PD. TTC PD is available as a longer-run alternative.

2Define portfolio and EAD

Enter the underlying portfolio, utilisation, undrawn CCF and effective number of obligors. These determine drawn guaranteed exposure, risk EAD and portfolio granularity.

3Set credit assumptions

LGD converts defaults into portfolio loss. Asset correlation controls default clustering. Maturity drives lifetime default accumulation and discounted lifetime ECL.

4Specify the legal waterfall

Set first-loss width, pari-passu share and stop-loss, or second-loss attachment and width. The same underlying loss distribution is passed through each contractual structure.

5Read ECL and capital separately

ECL is the probability-weighted expected claim. Economic capital covers unexpected tail loss above expected claim at the chosen confidence level.

6Read the fee on both bases

The quoted fee is a percentage of drawn guaranteed outstanding. The dashboard also shows the equivalent fee on the underlying portfolio for fair comparison across different tranche sizes.

Structure guide: what NCGCL is actually covering

First-loss guarantee

NCGCL absorbs initial portfolio losses from 0% to the selected width.
NCGCL first lossLender / residual loss
Illustration only; actual width is controlled by the slider.
Claim rate on guarantee = MIN(Portfolio loss, Width) / Width
  • Usually has the highest probability of being hit.
  • The quoted percentage can be high because it is charged on a relatively small first-loss tranche.
  • Compare the fee on the underlying portfolio before comparing it with pari-passu pricing.

Pari-passu guarantee

NCGCL shares losses proportionately with the lender, subject to the contractual claim cap.
Shared loss in coverage ratioAbove stop-loss
Nominal guarantee equals portfolio × coverage; the stop-loss is a cap on claim per guarantee.
Claim rate on guarantee = MIN(Portfolio loss rate, Stop-loss cap)
  • Every portfolio default can generate a proportionate claim.
  • Coverage changes the guarantee amount and fee amount; the rate on guarantee may remain broadly similar.
  • The stop-loss limits the legal maximum claim and economic capital.

Second-loss guarantee

The lender or another provider absorbs losses up to attachment; NCGCL covers the next layer.
First loss retainedNCGCL layerResidual loss
Detachment equals attachment plus tranche width.
Claim rate = MIN(MAX(Portfolio loss − Attachment, 0), Width) / Width
  • Expected loss can be low because ordinary losses remain below attachment.
  • Pricing is highly sensitive to correlation, confidence and attachment.
  • A low ECL does not necessarily mean low tail capital if clustered defaults can reach the layer.

Pricing and ECL methodology notes

1. PD selection and scenarios

The selected institution PD is the one-year risk anchor. The dashboard can use 2025 central PD, TTC PD, or the higher of the two. For MFBs, the COVID-adjusted central PD is also calculated.

Scenario weights and multipliers are policy assumptions for indicative screening; they should be recalibrated to transaction data and approved stress scenarios.

2. Correlated default distribution

The engine uses a finite-pool, one-factor Gaussian model. Asset correlation is not directly treated as default-event correlation. For each systematic state, a conditional default probability is calculated and then combined with a binomial distribution over the selected effective obligors.

q(z) = Φ[(Φ⁻¹(PD) − √ρ × z) / √(1 − ρ)]
Portfolio loss rate = Default rate × LGD

Higher correlation thickens the tail: the average PD is broadly unchanged, but clustered-loss outcomes become more likely.

3. Exposure at default and fee denominator

Nominal guarantee = Underlying portfolio × Structure coverage or tranche width
Risk EAD = Nominal guarantee × [Utilisation + (1 − Utilisation) × CCF]
Fee base = Nominal guarantee × Utilisation

ECL and capital use risk EAD, including the selected CCF for undrawn commitments. The annual fee is quoted on drawn guaranteed outstanding. This denominator distinction is why fee rates and fee amounts must be reviewed together.

4. Annual and lifetime ECL

Annual ECL = Probability-weighted expected claim rate × Risk EAD

Annual ECL combines the base, moderate and severe claim distributions. Lifetime ECL converts the annual PD into cumulative default probabilities, applies straight-line amortisation, reruns the legal waterfall for each year, and discounts incremental expected claims using the selected EIR.

Cumulative PD(y) = 1 − (1 − PD)ʸ
Lifetime ECL = Σ Incremental expected claim(y) / (1 + EIR)ʸ

This is an indicative lifetime-loss construct. It is not a complete IFRS 9 staging, macroeconomic scenario or contractual cash-shortfall engine.

5. Economic capital

Exact waterfall: calculate claim VaR from the chosen claim distribution, deduct expected claim, apply the model overlay, and cap capital at the remaining legal maximum claim.

EC exact = MIN(Max claim − EL, MAX[0, VaR(confidence) − EL] × Overlay)

IRB fallback: calculate an ASRF-style stressed underlying loss and pass that loss through the actual guarantee waterfall before deducting expected claim.

EC fallback = Waterfall(stressed portfolio loss) − Base expected claim

The selected capital method is then subject to the capital floor and legal maximum claim.

6. Indicative guarantee fee

ECL charge = Annual ECL / Fee base
Capital charge = Target RAROC × Selected EC / Fee base
Full fee = ECL charge + Capital charge + Administration + Funding + Risk margin

The actuarial floor contains ECL plus operating and risk loads but excludes return on economic capital. The full risk-based fee adds the target RAROC charge.

Probability of Default Framework
Core PD Construction
Measure the reported NPL stock rate.
NPL Ratioₜ = Classified Advancesₜ / Gross Advancesₜ
Annualize the multi-vintage stock.
PDₜ = 1 − (1 − NPL Ratioₜ)^(1 / L)
L = 3 years for banks and 2 years for MFBs in the central case.
Calculate a flow-based lower bound where feasible.
Flow PD LBₜ = MAX(0, ΔNPLₜ + Estimated Net Write-offsₜ) / Opening Performing Advances
Calculate through-the-cycle PD.
TTC PD = Annualized exposure-weighted NPL ratio across all available years
Combine sectors on performing exposure.
Combined PD = Σ(Performing Exposure × Sector PD) / Σ Performing Exposure

Critical limitations
Model governance: These PDs are top-down analytical proxies. For IFRS 9, regulatory capital, pricing or guarantee underwriting, they should be calibrated to internal obligor defaults, rating migrations, macroeconomic scenarios, LGD, EAD, maturity and transaction structure.
