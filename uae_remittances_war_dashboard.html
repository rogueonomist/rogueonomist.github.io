Analytical Brief — June 2026

UAE Remittances, the Iran War Shock & Capital Flight

A per-worker decomposition of Pakistan's remittance data (1974–May 2026) reveals that the surge from the UAE since March 2026 cannot be explained by wage income alone. Calibrated against a stock of 1.6 million Pakistani workers in the UAE, each worker sent home an average $375 above their normal three-month baseline — far exceeding typical monthly savings capacity. The residual, estimated at $500M after stripping festival effects, represents capital repatriation: accumulated savings, business cash balances, and financial assets being moved home.

Global Diaspora Overview

8.6 Million Pakistanis Abroad: Who Goes Where, and What They Send Back

Pakistan's 8.6 million overseas workers span 50+ countries, but the geography is strikingly concentrated. The six GCC states absorb ~5M workers (~58% of the total), while the UK, USA, EU, Canada and Australia together hold ~3M. The remittance story inverts this ratio: Western diaspora workers send 2–3× more per capita than Gulf workers, making Europe and North America high-yield corridors despite smaller headcounts.

Structural Insight
UAE's per-worker yield of $408/month in FY25 is structurally robust — up 42% from $288 in FY24. This pre-war acceleration itself requires explanation: Vision 2030 and UAE construction boom raised Pakistani worker wages; simultaneously, RDA adoption formalised channels. The FY25 $408/month norm is therefore the correct pre-war baseline from which war-period deviations are measured, not FY24's $288.
Seasonality Analysis

Decomposing Remittances: Trend, Season & Festival Effects

Remittance flows exhibit persistent monthly seasonality driven by Gulf employment cycles, Islamic festivals — Ramazan, Eid ul Fitr, Eid ul Azha — and Pakistan-specific calendar effects. A log-linear OLS model (2010–2025 training) captures all these simultaneously. The model achieves R²≈0.82 and reveals a 9% annual CAGR for UAE over 15 years.

FY26 Deep Dive

July 2025–May 2026: Pre-War Elevation, Then the March Break

FY26 UAE remittances were running above the long-run model even before the war — a structural level shift visible from July 2025. Then March 2026 produced a discontinuous upward jump, followed by April normalising somewhat, and May 2026 hitting an all-time record of $1.007B — the first time a single country has crossed $1B in a single month in Pakistan's remittance history.

War-Period Per-Capita Analysis

The Per-Worker Lens: From Aggregate Dollars to Individual Behaviour

Dividing UAE remittances by the calibrated worker stock (1.6M) converts aggregate dollar flows into a per-worker monthly signal. This is the most powerful diagnostic tool: if aggregate remittances spike but per-worker amounts are within plausible savings capacity, it could reflect more workers remitting. If per-worker amounts themselves spike well beyond savings capacity, it signals capital — not just wages — moving back.

The Smoking Gun
A UAE construction worker earns ~$500–700/month. After living costs (~$350), they can save $150–300/month. Their entire annual savings capacity is roughly $1,800–3,600. The 3-month excess of $375/worker is equivalent to 1–2.5 months of typical savings being liquidated in a single quarter. This points unambiguously to capital being moved, not just wages.

Capital Flight Estimation

Disaggregating the $600M Excess: Wages vs. Capital Repatriation

The aggregate $600M excess in UAE remittances over March–May 2026 has three potential components: (1) normal seasonal festival effects, (2) wage remittances above trend driven by higher employment or wages, and (3) capital repatriation — accumulated savings, business earnings, property proceeds, and liquid financial assets being moved to Pakistan for safety. The per-worker analysis allows us to disaggregate these with precision.

Central Finding
Of the $600M aggregate excess (Mar–May 2026), we estimate approximately $500M represents capital flight from the UAE Pakistani community — not regular wage income. The logic: (1) Saudi Arabia and other Gulf corridors showed only 10–16% YoY growth, consistent with wage-driven normality; (2) per-worker UAE amounts in May 2026 ($629) exceeded the total typical monthly income of most unskilled workers; (3) after stripping the Eid ul Azha festival premium (~$100M), ~$260M of May's excess alone has no seasonal explanation.

Capital Flight Proof Point
The FY25 blended average of $408/worker/month already fully depletes the implied average savings of a typical worker. Any sustained excess above this — such as the $94, $51, and $221 monthly surpluses in March through May 2026 — can only come from one of two sources: (1) liquidation of accumulated past savings held in UAE, or (2) transfer of capital assets (property deposits, business balances, investment accounts). Neither represents regular income remittances. This is definitionally capital flight.
Methodology & Caveats

Data Sources, Model Architecture & Limitations

Data Sources
(1) SBP Home Remittances Archive — monthly country-wise flows, July 1972 to May 2026, USD millions cash-flow basis. (2) BEOE/OEC Pakistan Workers Emigration Data — annual departures by country, 1971–April 2026 (54 countries, 14.8M cumulative departures). (3) Worker stock calibration: Saudi MoL (2023), UAE NBS (2023), UK ONS/Census (2021), US ACS (2022), StatsCan (2021), ABS (2021), Qatar PSA, Bahrain LMRA, UN DESA (2024). Structural break note: SBP switched from country-of-transaction to country-of-remitter in July 2019; RDA investment flows included from September 2020.

Seasonal Model
Log-linear OLS on 180 monthly observations (Jul 2010–Jun 2025): ln(UAE_t) = α + β·t + Σγ_m·I(m) + ε. Trend coefficient: +9.0%/yr. R²≈0.82. Seasonal factors normalised to mean=1.0. Eid ul Azha premium of ~$100M for May 2026 estimated from June data in 2023–2025.

Worker Stock Model
Survival-decay: Stock_2024 = Σ_τ Flow(τ) × (1−δ)^(2024−τ). Country-specific δ: Gulf states 18–22%, Western settled diaspora 5–6%, Malaysia/SEA 22%. Pre-2014 cohorts split into 1971–1990 (avg year 1981) and 1991–2013 (avg year 2002). Calibration multipliers derived from national census anchors. Coverage range: 1–2% (UK/USA) to 108% (Oman).
Key Caveats
(1) The $500M capital flight estimate is a central estimate; the range is $225M–$600M depending on which baseline is used. (2) UAE worker stock uncertainty is ±25% — the 1.6M calibrated figure could plausibly range from 1.3M to 1.9M; this shifts per-worker numbers but not aggregate totals. (3) The Eid ul Azha premium deduction ($100M) is estimated from limited comparable data (2023–2025 June Eid months only). (4) Pre-war FY26 remittances were already elevated, so the "new normal" baseline debate is genuine — the model correctly uses the long-run 2010–2025 fit as the primary baseline.
What This Analysis Does Not Claim
This analysis does not claim that (1) workers are leaving the UAE en masse — employment data does not yet confirm this; (2) all $500M is illegally moved capital — these are fully formal SBP-reported remittance channels; (3) the phenomenon will persist at this rate — it may normalise as war uncertainty recedes or escalates further. The finding is simply that the quantity and per-capita intensity of UAE remittances in March–May 2026 cannot be explained by wage income alone, and the residual is consistent with precautionary capital repatriation.
