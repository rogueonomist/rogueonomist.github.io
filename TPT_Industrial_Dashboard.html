Why Change Is Needed

Today's Electricity Bill Is Built on a Flawed Premise

Pakistan's industrial electricity tariff bundles everything into one price — fixed grid costs, fuel costs, capacity costs, and distribution charges — into a single per-unit (kWh) rate. This made sense when meters were simple. In 2025, it actively punishes industry.

The Core Insight
Think of it like a mobile data plan. Today, Pakistan's tariff charges you Rs 28 per MB whether you use data at 3am or peak hours. The TPT is like an "unlimited data" plan — you pay a fixed monthly fee upfront, then consume as much as you want at the real cost of delivery (Rs 8–9/unit). The more data you use, the lower your per-MB cost becomes.

The Duck Curve — Coming to Pakistan

Solar Is Already Reshaping Pakistan's Grid

As rooftop and utility-scale solar grow, daytime electricity supply exceeds demand in peak-solar hours, creating the now-famous "duck curve" — extremely cheap mid-day power followed by a sharp evening ramp. This is already visible in 2024–2025 ISMO data, and will intensify every year.

The Opportunity for Industry
Solar-hour electricity (roughly 8am–4pm) is the cheapest electricity Pakistan has ever had. Under the Two-Part Tariff, industries that shift operations to these hours pay dramatically less per unit. Understanding Cost Structure

Pakistan Has Built Rs 46,000+ per kW in Capacity — Most of It Fixed, Regardless of Use

The power sector's biggest challenge isn't fuel. It's that Rs 3 trillion of generation and grid infrastructure sits idle for much of the day, yet must be paid for through electricity bills. The key question is: how do we spread those fixed costs more fairly and efficiently?

What Is a "Fixed Cost"?

Imagine you own a factory building that costs Rs 1 million per year to maintain — whether you run one machine or a hundred. That is a fixed cost.

Power plants and grid infrastructure work the same way. Once built, they must be paid for every month — whether they generate 1 GWh or 10 GWh. Only fuel costs change with output.

Pakistan's power sector has Rs 44,944/kW/year in generation fixed costs alone. Added to DISCO distribution costs, the total reaches ~Rs 48,000–53,000/kW/year across the grid.

The Magic of Spreading Fixed Costs
If a factory pays Rs 100,000/month in fixed costs and produces 1,000 units, its fixed cost per unit is Rs 100. If it doubles production to 2,000 units, fixed cost per unit falls to Rs 50. The TPT makes this happen for electricity: run more, pay less per unit.

Why CSC Is More Efficient Than Bundled Pricing

The Capacity Service Charge (CSC) — A Smarter Way to Recover Fixed Costs

The Capacity Service Charge (CSC) is a monthly fixed charge per kW of your Maximum Demand Indicator (MDI) — essentially, your factory's subscribed capacity with the grid.

Think of it like your internet subscription: you pay a monthly fee for a 100 Mbps connection, regardless of whether you download 10 GB or 500 GB that month. The energy rate you pay on top covers only the actual cost of generating those kilowatt-hours.

Under the bundled tariff, fixed costs are bundled into the energy rate — which means factories that use more electricity are effectively cross-subsidizing idle capacity, and there is no incentive to optimize usage patterns.

The Proposed Framework

Two-Part Tariff: How It Works, Simply Explained

The Two-Part Tariff has two and only two components. Understand those two components, and you understand everything about your electricity bill under the new system.

⚡
Part 1
Capacity Service Charge (CSC)

A fixed monthly charge per kW of MDI. This pays your share of the grid's infrastructure — power plants, transmission lines, substations — that stand ready to serve you, whether you use them or not.

Proposed Rates (B3 Industrial)
Rs 6,301/kW/month
Example: 100 kW MDI = Rs 630,100/month fixed
🔌
Part 2
Variable Energy Rate (PRU)

A per-unit charge for actual electricity consumed, priced at true short-run marginal cost. No bundled fixed costs. No distortion. Just the real cost of generating those kilowatt-hours.

Proposed Rates (B3 Industrial, Time-of-Use)
Peak (evening): Rs 11.33/kWh
Off-peak (night): Rs 9.21/kWh
Solar hours (day): Rs 6.23/kWh
Total Monthly Bill Formula
Monthly Bill = (CSC × MDI) + (Variable Rate × kWh Consumed)

Example: 100 kW factory, 50,000 kWh/month (all off-peak):
= (Rs 6,301 × 100) + (Rs 9.21 × 50,000) = Rs 630,100 + Rs 460,500 = Rs 1,090,600/month
Effective rate = Rs 21.8/kWh vs. Rs 28/kWh bundled tariff = 22% savings

Optimization Strategies

Four Ways Industries Can Cut Their Electricity Bill Under TPT

1
Add a Second or Third Shift
More production hours mean more kWh consumed, spreading the CSC fixed charge over more units. Each additional unit costs only Rs 8–9, far below the Rs 28 bundled rate.
2
Shift to Solar Hours
Run energy-intensive processes between 8am–4pm during summer, when the ToU solar-hour rate is Rs 6.23/kWh — less than a quarter of the current bundled rate.
3
Manage Your Peak Demand (MDI)
The CSC is based on MDI. Stagger machine start-ups, use inverter drives, or schedule high-draw equipment sequentially to reduce your peak demand and lower your monthly CSC.
4
Add Battery Storage
Charge batteries during cheap solar hours (Rs 6.23/kWh), discharge them to reduce MDI during evening peaks. Double benefit: lower CSC + lower energy cost.

The Problem with the Old Tariff in a Solar World
As solar drives midday prices toward zero, a bundled tariff becomes increasingly irrational — consumers would be paying Rs 28/unit for power that costs Rs 3/unit to generate. The TPT automatically passes these solar-hour cost savings directly to industrial consumers through Time-of-Use rates. Every year that solar expands, the solar-hour rate drops further.
Problem: Duck Curve Creates Instability

As solar floods the grid midday, demand must be actively managed. The grid faces two crises daily: over-supply at noon (solar surplus must be curtailed or absorbed) and under-supply at 7–10pm (evening ramp as solar disappears and demand spikes).

In California — which experienced this first — unconstrained duck curves led to negative electricity prices at noon and emergency power shortfalls in the evening. Pakistan is on the same trajectory.

Solution: TPT Turns Industry into a Grid Asset

Under TPT with solar-hour ToU rates, industries voluntarily shift consumption to midday — absorbing the solar surplus, reducing curtailment, and flattening the duck curve.

This doesn't require mandates or regulations. It happens naturally through price signals: Rs 6.23/kWh in solar hours vs Rs 11.33/kWh in peak hours. A 5,000 kW cement plant shifting 30% of load to solar hours saves Rs 15+ million annually while absorbing grid surplus.

Quick Reference: Everything You Need to Know

Frequently Asked Questions
Will my bill go up?
Not if you maintain the same consumption. The Revenue Neutrality clause (MIN formula) ensures your bill cannot exceed the current bundled tariff. If you grow consumption above the break-even threshold (~36% load factor), your bill goes down.
What is MDI and why does it matter?
MDI (Maximum Demand Indicator) is the highest 15-minute average power draw your factory recorded in a given month, measured in kW. Under TPT, your CSC is multiplied by this number. Reducing your peak demand reduces your fixed bill — even if total kWh consumed stays constant.
What is the "break-even" load factor?
Load factor = (kWh consumed) ÷ (MDI × 720 hours). Break-even is ~36%, meaning if you run your machines at ~36% of peak capacity on average throughout the month, your TPT bill equals your current bundled bill. Above 36%, TPT wins.
Is CSC adjusted quarterly like FCA?
No. The CSC is set annually by NEPRA to provide investment certainty. Only the variable energy rate follows quarterly FCA/QTA cycles, aligned with fuel cost changes. This means factories can plan multi-year capital projects with a predictable fixed cost component.
Can I opt out later?
The proposal includes an opt-in structure for Phase 1, allowing industrial consumers to switch to TPT at any point. Transition back to bundled tariff provisions are under regulatory discussion with NEPRA.
Does TPT benefit new investment decisions?
Significantly. A greenfield factory planning capacity knows that if it runs at 50%+ load factor, it will pay 25–30% less for electricity than under the current tariff. This changes the economics of capacity expansion, shift-work, and automation investment throughout Pakistan's industrial sector.

Glossary
MDI — Maximum Demand Indicator
Highest 15-minute average kW recorded in the billing month. The basis for the CSC charge.
CSC — Capacity Service Charge
Monthly fixed charge per kW of MDI, covering grid infrastructure costs.
SRMC — Short-Run Marginal Cost
The variable cost of generating one additional kWh — primarily fuel cost. The basis for TPT energy rates.
Load Factor
kWh consumed ÷ (MDI × 720). Measures how efficiently you use your subscribed capacity. Higher = better under TPT.
ToU — Time-of-Use
Electricity rates that vary by time of day, reflecting actual generation costs. Solar hours are cheapest; evening peak is most expensive.
Duck Curve
The U-shaped daily demand curve caused by solar flooding the grid midday, creating cheap daytime power and expensive evening ramps.
RNR — Revenue Neutrality Rider
Mechanism ensuring DISCOs recover their required revenue even if adoption rates or consumption changes reduce collections.
BESS — Battery Energy Storage System
Behind-the-meter batteries that charge during cheap solar hours and discharge during expensive peak hours or to shave MDI spikes.
FCA — Fuel Cost Adjustment
Quarterly adjustment to variable energy rates reflecting actual fuel costs. TPT variable rates follow FCA; CSC does not.
