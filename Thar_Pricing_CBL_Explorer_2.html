Methodology 1
Short-Run Marginal Cost
Price equals the variable cost of producing one additional tonne using existing installed capacity. Fixed costs are treated as already sunk and recovered through the IPP channel.
Principle: Because TNPTL, TEL, and LEPCL recover the full $19.23/tonne capacity payment through the MYT tariff, any non-IPP tonne requires zero contribution to legacy fixed costs. Price = variable cost only.

Methodology 2
Long-Run Marginal Cost
Price equals the full cost — variable plus capital — of supplying one additional tonne through the next increment of new capacity. Ensures Phase III expansion earns its required return.
Principle: Phase III expansion (7.6 → 11.2 MTPA) adds 3.6 MTPA at USD 66.78M incremental capex (~USD 18.55M/MTPA). LRMC = SRMC + annualised Phase III fixed cost per tonne.

Methodology 3
Ramsey Pricing
Maximises total economic welfare by charging mark-ups above marginal cost that are inversely proportional to price elasticity. Inelastic users (IPPs) carry a higher burden; elastic users (cement, chemicals) receive near-marginal prices.
Rule: (P − MC)/P = λ / |ε|. Where λ (Ramsey number) is calibrated to revenue adequacy. Lower elasticity = higher price.
P_i = MC ÷ (1 − λ/|ε_i|) [where |ε_i| > λ]
Else: P = Full tariff (capped at $34.77)

Methodology 4
Two-Part Tariff
Industrial user pays a fixed annual subscription covering their Phase III capacity share (regardless of volume actually lifted), plus a variable charge per tonne at SRMC. Provides SECMC with cost recovery certainty and the user with a predictable fuel price.
Key correction: The fixed subscription component must cover only Phase III-specific fixed costs (USD 3.47/tonne at contracted volume) — NOT the legacy Phase I–II capacity payment (USD 19.23/tonne), which is already recovered through IPPs.

Methodology 5
Club Goods / Postage Stamp
A defined tranche of Phase III capacity is designated for industrial users. Club members share the fixed cost of that tranche equally per unit of contracted volume, while paying SRMC per actual tonne lifted.
Design: All club members pay the same per-tonne fixed charge, regardless of volume tier. More members = lower per-member fixed payment. Each member also pays SRMC variable charge. Default tranche: 2.0 MTPA of Phase III capacity.
Tranche capex = (Tranche MTPA / 3.6) × USD 66.78M
Annual tranche charge = Tranche capex × CRF
Per-member fixed = Annual charge ÷ N members
Per-tonne fixed = Per-member fixed ÷ member volume

Methodology 6
Volume-Tiered Pricing
Per-tonne price declines as contracted volume increases. Directly internalises scale economics — larger commitments receive more fixed cost dilution benefit. Tiers are self-selecting by revealed preference.
Design: Five tiers, each with a fixed cost contribution fraction applied to the Phase III-specific fixed cost pool. Larger volume = lower fixed allocation per tonne. Variable SRMC paid on actual lift.
Tier price = SRMC + (tier_fixed_fraction × max_fixed)

Verdict
Most practical as the immediate commercial framework. Cement plants in trial phase pay $24–27/tonne; anchor urea/chemical investors committing ≥ 3 MTPA pay $15–17/tonne (LRMC). Self-selecting — no need for TCEB to estimate elasticities ex-ante.

Methodology 7
Import Parity Cap
Price is set at energy-equivalent import parity less a 10–15% discount — ensuring Thar always undercuts the alternative. Operator's margin is the spread above SRMC. Price adjusts automatically with global markets.
Formula: Non-IPP ceiling = Import_parity_equivalent × (1 − discount%). Energy adjustment: Thar (2,700 kcal/kg) vs imported (5,200 kcal/kg GAR). The import price slider above drives all calculations in real time.
Energy equiv. Thar price = Import_price × (2700 / 5200)
Ceiling = Energy_equiv × (1 − 0.12 discount)
Operator margin = Ceiling − SRMC

How Power Consumer Savings Accrue
The mechanism: Every non-IPP tonne of Thar coal sold from Phase IV+ production (to cement plants, converted power plants, urea/chemical complexes) carries the Consumer Benefit Levy (CBL) set above. CBL × non-IPP volume = a fund that directly reduces the coal tariff paid by TNPTL, TEL, and LEPCL — passing through NEPRA's quarterly FCA into lower electricity bills. The Compounding Effect
Each year of non-IPP market development adds another tranche of CBL revenue. By 2035 (V₁ = 25 MTPA), the fully ramped consumer benefit fund reaches $3.6M/year at $1/t CBL — or $18M/year at $5/t. This compounds over the 30-year MYT life: cumulative national saving from 2027–2031 alone is significant, and grows thereafter at full V₁ run-rate.

ROE + ROEDC Coverage — The Equity Payback Equation

Power consumers currently pay $56.00M/year in equity returns embedded in the MYT coal tariff — $34.12M/year for Phase I (20% IRR on $152.8M equity, confirmed from COD determination) and $21.88M/year for Phase II (20% IRR on $42.1M equity). Phase III carries zero equity cost — the 7.6→11.2 MTPA expansion was financed entirely through operational cash flows and Meezan Bank debt at KIBOR+1.85%. The optimal CBL is the per-tonne levy that, at a given non-IPP production volume, exactly offsets these equity payments — so the non-IPP market effectively repays the equity burden carried by power consumers.
