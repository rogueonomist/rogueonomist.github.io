iIn plain English
Pakistan's wheat system has a nitrogen productivity problem — not a nitrogen quantity problem. Since 1981, Pakistan has increased nitrogen applied to wheat by +267% while wheat yield grew only +98%. The result: every kilogram of nitrogen now produces roughly 18 kg of wheat, down from 34 kg in 1981. Pakistan applies as much nitrogen per hectare as China, but gets less than half China's yield. The evidence points to seed quality, sowing timeliness, nutrient imbalance and agronomic management as the binding constraints — not nitrogen shortage.

Central Finding: Pakistan has a nitrogen conversion problem, not a nitrogen supply problem

The evidence is clear: Pakistan is applying sufficient or even excess nitrogen per hectare by international standards. The constraint is the system's ability to convert that nitrogen into grain. Wheat yield per kg of applied nitrogen has fallen 46% since 1981. The three principal explanations are: (1) declining marginal N response as N intensity has outrun complementary inputs; (2) severe nutrient imbalance — phosphorus application has collapsed relative to nitrogen, breaking the N:P ratio needed for efficient N uptake; and (3) agronomic execution gaps — late sowing, weak seed genetics, poor root development and inadequate water management all reduce the wheat plant's ability to utilize applied N. Adding more nitrogen without fixing these constraints will continue to produce diminishing returns.

The diminishing returns finding

In two of the last four 5-year windows (2006–11 and 2016–21), marginal N productivity was zero or negative. This does not mean nitrogen has zero effect — it means that in those specific periods, the factors constraining yield (weather, seed, management, water, late sowing) were more binding than nitrogen supply. Adding more N in such conditions does not raise yield; it raises cost. The 2001–06 anomaly (+30.7) reflects the exceptionally high yield response in a period of variety adoption alongside moderate N growth, not a reversal of the trend.

!Critical constraint
Nitrogen does not act in isolation. Wheat's uptake of N depends on adequate phosphorus availability — P is essential for root development and the biochemical pathways that fix N into protein. When P is deficient, added N cannot be efficiently converted to grain. Pakistan's N:P ratio has deteriorated from a historically high 5–6:1 in the 1980s to the recommended 2:1 in the 2000s, and then worsened again to 4.9:1 in 2022-23 — suggesting a severe recent pullback in phosphorus application that is almost certainly reducing N response efficiency. The recommended N:P ratio for wheat in Pakistan is approximately 2:1 (N:P₂O₅).

The 2022-23 phosphate collapse: P₂O₅ offtake fell from 1,093 kt in 2021-22 to 734 kt in 2022-23 — a 33% single-year decline — driven primarily by the extreme increase in DAP prices following global fertilizer price spikes and PKR devaluation. This means wheat planted in the 2022-23 Rabi season was severely phosphorus-deficient. Nitrogen applied to P-deficient soil produces less grain per unit of N. The recovery in 2023-24 (DAP offtake +41.6% per NFDC) should partially correct this, but soil P depletion effects take multiple seasons to reverse.

Methodology
Core methodological requirement: PFPN is calculated using nitrogen applied specifically to wheat, not total national nitrogen consumption. Total N is allocated to wheat using a 42.5% base-case share (sensitivity: 40–45%), consistent with FAO/NFDC crop-specific fertilizer use surveys and the Competition Commission of Pakistan's crop-wise nutrient data showing wheat at 50% of total nutrients and approximately 42–45% of total N specifically.
Pre-2014 N reconstruction (1981-82 to 2013-14): National total N offtake estimated from four anchor points: (1) FAO/NFDC: 1Mt total nutrients in 1980/81 → 780 kt N; (2) 2Mt in 1992/93 → 1,560 kt N; (3) 3Mt in 2002/03 → 2,340 kt N; (4) IOP Science (Alam et al. 2018 citing IFA 2016): 3,852 kt total nutrients in 2012-13 → 3,005 kt N. Annual values interpolated using cubic spline. N = 78% of total nutrients throughout (NFDC historical average). Estimates are flagged as reconstructed in the data table.

Post-2014 N calculation (2014-15 to 2025-26): Computed directly from company-level monthly fertilizer offtake records (project files, January 2014 – March 2026) using: N = (Urea × 0.46) + (DAP × 0.18) + (CAN × 0.26) + (NP × 0.22) + (AS × 0.21) + (MAP × 0.12). Aggregated to fiscal year (July–June).

Wheat production/area/yield: Agriculture Statistics Pakistan (1981-82 to 2022-23); Economic Survey 2025-26 for 2025-26 (29.61 Mt, 9.48 Mha); 2023-24 and 2024-25 estimated.

Key uncertainty: The wheat-specific N allocation (42.5% base case) is the most significant source of uncertainty in this analysis. The FAO survey (2004, data from ~2002) found wheat = 45% of total nutrients; more recent PBS/CCP data suggests 50% of total fertilizer products. Applying 42.5% to N specifically (lower than 45% of all nutrients) is conservative and reflects that wheat has a higher N:P application ratio than some competing crops, so its share of N alone is likely slightly below its share of total nutrients. The sensitivity analysis (40–45% band) bounds this uncertainty. PFPN conclusions are directionally identical across all three cases.
