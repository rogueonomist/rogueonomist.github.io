Forward Curve — Key Finding
The base-case forward curve projects average MC of 17.8 PKR/kWh and AFC of 7.5 PKR/kWh over May 2026 – April 2027. Demand seasonality drives a dual-peak structure: a summer peak (Jun–Jul: MC ~18–19 PKR/kWh) and a winter peak (Jan: ~25 PKR/kWh, driven by RLNG heating load and low hydro). The AFC curve stays structurally compressed vs MC — averaging 2.2–2.4× below MC — as nuclear, hydro, and expanding solar remain the base-load anchors. For SRMC-based tariff crediting (e.g., net metering or prosumer export premiums), the AFC forward of 5.2 – 9.5 PKR/kWh provides the lower bound, while MC of 14.5 – 25.0 PKR/kWh sets the demand-management value ceiling.

Pakistan's fuel cost exhibits pronounced bi-modal seasonality. Primary summer demand peak (May–Aug) brings high system loads of 16,000–21,000 MW driven by cooling load, but is partially offset by hydro availability (monsoon) and expanded solar — keeping AFC relatively compressed despite MC elevation. A secondary January peak emerges from RLNG heating demand against low river flows, producing the year's highest MC readings (21–27 PKR/kWh). Winter troughs (Nov, Feb) have the lowest MC and narrowest MC/AFC spreads.

Three scenarios bracket the forward curve: Base case assumes a PKR/USD rate of 280, RLNG at $10.5/mmBTU, and a normal monsoon season. Upside stress models a currency weakening to PKR 295–305 and RLNG at $13–14/mmBTU (LNG market spike or procurement failure). Downside relief assumes better-than-average hydro inflows (+10%), RLNG at $9/mmBTU, and domestic gas availability improving via new well connections.

Risk Assessment for Tariff Design
The upside stress scenario produces a 32% increase in average MC (to ~23.5 PKR/kWh) and 26% increase in AFC (to ~9.5 PKR/kWh) relative to base case. Critically, the January RLNG spike under stress reaches 32+ PKR/kWh — comparable to the Jan-26 actual of 26.5 PKR/kWh elevated by a further RLNG shock. For the PPMC V8 Two-Part Tariff framework, SRMC crediting for prosumer exports should be anchored to the AFC base forward (5.2–9.5 PKR/kWh) rather than spot MC to avoid windfall exposure. The proposed Rs 7.50/kWh export premium sits within the AFC range for most months, making it defensible as a transitional prosumer incentive rate.

Solar penetration growth and hydro availability shape the AFC independently of thermal pricing.

Tariff Design Implication — R-TPT Prosumer Crediting
The proposed Rs 7.50/kWh solar export premium sits within or above the forward AFC range for 9 of 12 months (May-26 to Apr-27 base case AFC: 5.2–12.5 PKR/kWh). This validates the premium as a cost-reflective incentive: it exceeds pure SRMC crediting (AFC floor) while remaining below peak-hour MC in high-demand months. For industrial TPT, the energy rate (PRU) should track the V8 QTA cycle. The forward MC of 17–19 PKR/kWh in peak summer supports keeping PRU at its current band. The CSC/HCC fixed component should remain stable and QTA-excluded — the forward curve confirms that variable fuel costs carry sufficient volatility to be separately managed.
