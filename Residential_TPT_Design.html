Core finding: MDI reduction under R-TPT is worth PKR 10,085 per kW per month. For a 10 kW SL household, reducing evening grid peak from 8 kW to 3 kW saves PKR 50,425/month — dwarfing any benefit from BESS energy arbitrage (Rs 6.23 solar → Rs 11.33 peak spread = Rs 1,346/month for a 10 kWh battery). ☀️
Type A — Net Metering Prosumer
Rooftop solar with bidirectional meter. Exports surplus to grid. Currently billed on net units. Largest and fastest-growing cohort.
Current tariff benefit: Export credited at retail Rs 37.92/kWh
R-TPT challenge: CSC on full SL regardless of net consumption
R-TPT opportunity: Low energy rates during solar hours; export + MDI management
Key metric: Gross import MDI (evening draw). Keep it low = keep CSC low
🔋
Type B — Behind-the-Meter Solar (No Export)
Rooftop solar, unidirectional or standard meter. Self-consumes all solar; surplus wasted or stored. Many BTM installations are this type.
Current tariff benefit: Reduces grid draw → lower volumetric bill
R-TPT challenge: CSC based on MDI — evening peak still high without BESS
R-TPT opportunity: With AMI + BESS, can manage gross import MDI actively
Key metric: Evening grid peak. Solar helps daytime; BESS needed for evening MDI control
⚡
Type C — Solar + BESS (Integrated System)
Rooftop solar with home battery. Self-consumes solar, stores surplus, discharges battery at evening peak to avoid grid draw. Optimal R-TPT positioning.
Current tariff benefit: Near-zero bills; solar + BESS eliminates most grid draw
R-TPT challenge: Must pay CSC even if grid draw is minimal
R-TPT opportunity: BESS dramatically reduces MDI → huge CSC savings at 20% SL floor
Key metric: Gross import MDI. Well-managed BESS can reduce to 20% SL = minimum CSC floor
Why these consumers are the opt-in population: Consumers who have already invested in solar and/or BESS have demonstrated price sensitivity and willingness to manage energy use. They are the most likely to opt in to R-TPT. Yet the V8 design as written would cause them the most harm. Getting the design right for these consumers is essential for opt-in adoption and system-wide peak reduction goals.

The SL Floor Problem — and Solution
The 50% SL floor kills BESS investment incentives below the threshold
50%
V8 Current Design
All consumers: billing demand = max(50% SL, MDI). For a 10kW SL house: minimum CSC = 10,085 × 5 = PKR 50,425/month. MDI management below 5kW has zero value — kills BESS incentive in the key range.
35%
Proposed: Solar-Only Prosumers
Net metering or BTM solar, no BESS: floor = 35% SL. For 10kW SL: minimum CSC = 10,085 × 3.5 = PKR 35,298/month. Acknowledges that solar genuinely reduces peak grid dependency. Still fair network cost contribution.
20%
Proposed: Solar+BESS Consumers
Verified solar + BESS with AMI: floor = 20% SL. For 10kW SL: minimum CSC = 10,085 × 2 = PKR 20,170/month. BESS investment creates PKR 30,255/month saving vs V8 design. Simple payback on BESS under 18 months.

Strategic Playbooks
How each consumer type maximises benefit under R-TPT
☀️
Type A: Net-Metering Only (No BESS)
Primary lever is evening MDI management — reduce household peak draw between 19:00–23:00. Practical steps: run AC, washing machine, geysers before 19:00; use LED bulbs and fans during peak; avoid coincident high-draw appliances in same hour.

Secondary lever: maximize solar self-consumption vs export. Under R-TPT, solar self-consumption avoids paying Rs 6.23–9.21/kWh. Under current tariff, export at retail was more valuable. Now self-consume as much as possible.
Achievable benefit: Reducing evening MDI from 6kW to 4kW saves PKR 20,170/month in CSC under proposed 35% floor design. No capital expenditure required.
🔋
Type B: BTM Solar (No Export)
Under current tariff, BTM solar has good economics: avoid Rs 37.92/kWh on every self-consumed unit. Under R-TPT, the volumetric savings diminish (avoiding Rs 6.23–11.33/kWh) but the MDI management opportunity opens up.

Adding BESS makes this consumer type highly attractive: solar charges BESS during day, BESS discharges at peak to cap MDI. Achievable MDI reduction of 3–4 kW with a 10 kWh battery. This qualifies for the 20% SL floor.
Key decision: Adding a 10 kWh BESS (≈ PKR 400,000) enables Rs 30,255/month in CSC savings → payback in 13 months. R-TPT makes BESS dramatically more attractive than under current tariff.
⚡
Type C: Solar + BESS (Integrated)
This is the optimal R-TPT configuration. Dispatch strategy: charge BESS from solar 10:00–16:00 (Rs 6.23/kWh or free if own solar). Discharge during peak 19:00–23:00 to limit grid draw to MDI floor.

Target: Gross import MDI ≤ 20% SL. For 10kW SL household, target ≤ 2 kW grid draw at any moment — achievable with 6 kW solar + 10 kWh BESS. CSC = 10,085 × 2 = PKR 20,170/month vs V8 as-designed PKR 80,680.
Grid benefit: This consumer becomes a firm demand-response asset. Peak reduction of 5–8 kW per household × millions of households = GW-scale peak flattening. Enables lower installed capacity and lower QTA for all consumers.
📱
BESS Dispatch Intelligence
Under R-TPT, the MDI for billing is the single highest 15-minute peak gross import during the month. This means one outlier event (e.g., AC + geyser + EV charging simultaneously for 15 minutes) determines the entire month's CSC.

Smart BESS dispatch algorithms that monitor real-time grid import and limit peak draw are therefore extremely high-value. This creates a market for home energy management systems (HEMS) and smart inverters that can provide MDI capping as a service.
Market signal: R-TPT creates a PKR 10,085/kW/month market for MDI management technology. This is a powerful incentive for smart inverter, HEMS, and demand response product development in Pakistan.

ToU Rate Structure — Domestic (V8 Official)
Why BESS + Solar is the System's Best Friend

Pakistan's duck curve (V8 slides 5–7) shows solar demand trough of -15.4% during solar hours (2023→2025), with evening peak SRMC of Rs 12.7–23.41/kWh. The R-TPT ToU structure mirrors this exactly:

• Solar hours (10–16h): Rs 6.23/kWh — cheapest. Grid has surplus. Solar + BESS should charge.

• Peak (19–23h): Rs 11.33/kWh — most expensive. Grid is stressed. BESS should discharge.

• Non-solar (rest): Rs 9.21/kWh — baseline. Manage grid imports here.

A household with Solar+BESS that follows this dispatch pattern absorbs surplus solar midday AND reduces evening peak draw — exactly what the system needs. The BESS MDI management floor (20% SL) is the pricing mechanism that makes this economically rational.

Bill Breakdown — Four Scenarios Compared
Implementation
From V8 industrial launch to solar-optimised full residential R-TPT by FY2029
Phase 0 — Q4 FY2026 (Now)
V8 industrial consultation + prosumer registration launch
V8 TPT consultation concluded. NEPRA determination for industrial/agricultural. Solar system and BESS registration database established with DISCOs. All net-metering connections flagged for Phase-1 prosumer HCC. Consumer communication launched. RNR framework submitted.
Phase 1 — July 2026
Industrial opt-in live; Flat prosumer HCC; Standard R-TPT for high-use domestic
V8 TPT live for B1–B4 industrial and agricultural. Residential: flat HCC Rs 400/kW-SL for all prosumers (solar/BESS/NM). Phase-1 tiered HCC for standard domestic tiers T0–T4a. Export premium Rs 7.50/kWh for net-metering consumers. MIN(Bundled, R-TPT) floor for T0–T3. BESS registration process opened.
Phase 2 — FY2027
AMI rollout; Tiered SL floors activated; Full solar/BESS economics
AMI mandated for: all A-1b consumers (>700u); all net-metering prosumers; all registered BESS owners. Solar-only floor activated at 35% SL (from 50%). Solar+BESS floor activated at 20% SL for AMI-verified consumers. Gross import billing replaces net billing for prosumers. Monthly MDI-based CSC billing activated.
Phase 3 — FY2028
Standard tier AMI; HCC step-up; HEMS market development
A-1a Standard tier (201–400u) offered opt-in AMI. Phase-1 flat HCC rates increased 50–75% toward cost-reflective levels. Home Energy Management System (HEMS) integration protocol published — allows certified HEMS to guarantee MDI caps and qualify for 20% SL floor without manual verification. BISP direct subsidy replaces tariff cross-subsidy for lifeline consumers.
Phase 4 — FY2029+
Full cost-reflective R-TPT across all domestic consumers
Full V8 CSC of 10,085/kW with modified SL floors (50%/35%/20%) applied universally. Energy at pure domestic SRMC. Cross-subsidy fully replaced by fiscal transfers. Pakistan residential sector achieves economically efficient tariff structure with embedded solar/BESS investment incentives.
