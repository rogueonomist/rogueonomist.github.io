Central Finding
Pakistan's formal diesel market broke structurally in 2023 and has never fully recovered. The pre-break diesel-LSM relationship — with a 12-month rolling correlation of 0.82 and an elasticity of 1.13 — implies formal HSD should be significantly higher than observed across 41 of the last ~65 months. The cumulative formal-market gap represents a Rs 229bn PDL hole for the state, a Rs 1,286bn informal diesel economy operating outside regulated channels, and an indicative Rs 42.2bn windfall for pump operators handling displaced volume. The April-May 2026 data — with May at a formalization ratio of 52.5%, concurrent with PDL near-abolition — represents the most acute episode of formal-market stress in the dataset. This is not a demand-side problem. It is a supply-chain integrity problem requiring structural reform across pricing, logistics, levies, and enforcement.

Aggregate Fiscal & Commercial Losses
Who Pays for the Leakage?
The fiscal burden of Pakistan's diesel leakage falls on three parties: the state (lost PDL/PL revenue), formal OMC operators (lost volume and market share), and ultimately the formal diesel consumer (who faces progressively higher PDL as the government tries to compensate for base erosion). The pump and informal channel operator is the structural beneficiary.

The PDL trap: Each year the state lost PDL revenue (2023: Rs 70.7bn; 2024: Rs 69.6bn; 2025: Rs 55.7bn), it responded by raising the PDL rate on the remaining formal volume — from Rs 32.5/L in Jan-23, to Rs 50/L in Apr-23, to Rs 60/L in Dec-23, to Rs 73–77/L by mid-2025. This is a classic pro-cyclical revenue substitution: the state taxes formal consumers more heavily to compensate for informal market erosion, which in turn raises the price arbitrage incentive and drives more leakage. The abolition of PDL in April 2026 broke this cycle but simultaneously triggered the most severe leakage episode in the dataset.

Market fragmentation amplifies leakage risk: With 34 licensed OMCs competing under fixed margins (OMC: Rs 10.50/L, dealer: Rs 12.50/L), smaller operators face commercial pressure to source cheaper informal product. Fixed margins eliminate any competitive incentive to reduce costs through logistics efficiency. The informal market's price advantage (Iranian diesel at Rs 100–150+ below formal prices) is so large that it structurally undermines the economics of formal compliance for smaller OMCs and pump operators. Margin deregulation combined with supply-chain traceability is the commercial counterpart to enforcement.
Model Architecture
How the Counterfactual is Constructed
The model does not directly observe smuggled diesel. It observes formal OMC HSD sales and compares them against what the historical diesel-LSM relationship — controlling for industrial activity and seasonal patterns — would imply. The gap is called "potential displaced diesel" or "formal-market leakage," not proven smuggling.
Model: log(HSD salest) = α + β · log(LSMIt) + Σ γm · Monthm,t + εt

Fit period: January 2021 – December 2022 (pre-break baseline)
Estimated elasticity (β): ≈ 1.13 | R-squared: ≈ 0.69

Formalization ratio = (Actual HSDt / Counterfactual HSDt) × 100
Potential displaced volume = max(Counterfactual HSDt − Actual HSDt, 0)
Flagged threshold: Formalization ratio < 95%
Key caveat: The model cannot distinguish between smuggling, informal sales, price-driven demand destruction, inventory drawdowns, fuel switching to LNG/biomass, efficiency improvements, and construction/agriculture sector weakness. When diesel falls more than implied, the model registers a "leakage signal" — not a smuggling confession. The 2023 Reuters reporting and IMF/SBP commentary on Iranian fuel triangulation provide external triangulation, but the model itself is agnostic about the source of the gap.

Seasonality
Diesel and LSM Have Different Seasonal Profiles
Diesel peaks in April–May (agriculture, construction, pre-monsoon logistics) and October–November (post-harvest, winter freight). LSM peaks in January–February and December. A counterfactual that ignores this divergence would wrongly classify normal seasonal diesel weakness as leakage. Month fixed effects are therefore essential to the model's validity.

Structural Reform Framework
Five Pillars to Restore Formal Market Integrity and Reduce Pump Prices
Pakistan's diesel leakage is not primarily an enforcement problem — it is a price-arbitrage problem created by a broken pricing formula, an opaque freight structure, a punitive and unstable levy regime, and the absence of a strategic buffer. Closing the formalization gap requires simultaneous action across five tracks. Enforcement without pricing reform will fail; pricing reform without enforcement creates a different kind of leakage.
Estimated total pump price reduction potential: Rs 60–68/litre over 36 months, achievable without blanket subsidy, without undermining refinery economics, and without a permanent fiscal hole — if all five pillars are executed concurrently. Each pillar independently is insufficient; the system effect requires overlap.
Pillar 01 — Pricing Formula
Replace Import-Parity with Weighted National Supply Cost
Pakistan's current formula prices all diesel as if it arrived by tanker — despite 80% of HSD being locally refined. This embeds phantom ocean freight, marine insurance, and LC margins into every litre that was never imported. The fix is a Weighted National Supply Cost (WNSC) formula that separates local and imported supply costs.
HSD price = (0.80 × Local HSD cost) + (0.20 × Imported landed cost) + IFEM + Margins + Levies
Petrol price = (0.33 × Local MS cost) + (0.67 × Imported landed cost) + IFEM + Margins + Levies
Local shares updated quarterly from OCAC/OGRA production data. Refinery economics are explicitly protected — the formula removes phantom freight, not refinery gate pricing.

Pillar 02 — Long-Term Supply
G2G Term Supply: Cap Import Premiums, End Spot Default
Pakistan buys residual import volumes on spot markets by default — absorbing premium spikes of USD 10–30/bbl above Platts benchmarks. Structured 5-year framework agreements with Saudi Arabia (Aramco Trading), Kuwait (KPC), UAE (ADNOC), and Oman (OQ) with a premium cap mechanism eliminate this volatility.
Product price = Benchmark + min(Market Premium, Agreed Cap)
At USD 7/bbl cap vs USD 15/bbl spot: saves USD 8/bbl/cargo
At USD 10/bbl cap vs USD 30/bbl spike: saves USD 20/bbl
Every USD 1/bbl saving ≈ Rs 1.75/litre at the pump. A USD 10/bbl cap saving = Rs 17–18/litre retail reduction. Terms should include 90–180 day deferred payment and emergency cargo call options.

Pillar 03 — IFEM Unbundling
Five-Zone Competitive Freight Model: End the Blind Pool
The current pooled IFEM (~Rs 16.78/L) averages freight costs nationally — protecting inefficient road transport and disincentivizing pipeline utilization. The reform creates five zones, each with a maximum allowed logistics cost, while preserving explicit support for remote areas.

Pillar 04 — PDL Structural Reform
Split the Lazy Tax: Four Gazetted Components with Countercyclical Rule
The PDL has become a discretionary fiscal plug — easy to collect, adjustable fortnightly, and applied with no rules and no purpose-linking. At Rs 60–77/L (before 2026 abolition), it was larger than OMC margins, IFEM, and dealer margins combined. The reform splits it into four transparent, purpose-linked components:
  • Road User Levy — Annual statutory instrument; funds NHA/provincial roads
  • Carbon/Climate Levy — EV conversion, public bus electrification; preset annual schedule
  • Strategic Reserve Levy — Ring-fenced SPR fund; expires at 30–45 day cover target
  • Stabilisation Levy — Countercyclical: falls when landed cost rises, rises when cost falls. Formula: SL(t) = Base − α × (LandedCost(t) − Reference). Cap and floor by statute.

Pillar 05 — Strategic Storage
Three-Layer Buffer: Commercial → Bonded → Sovereign SPR
Pakistan has no meaningful buffer between the international market and the retail pump. Every supply disruption — port congestion, tanker delay, refinery outage — triggers panic spot-buying and hoarding. The fix is a three-layer architecture with legally mandated release triggers and replenishment obligations.

Pillar 06 — Anti-Leakage Enforcement Architecture
Monthly Formalization Dashboard + Margin Deregulation + Supply-Chain Traceability
The five structural pillars above reduce the economic incentive to source informal diesel. But enforcement must close the residual gap. Three mechanisms are required simultaneously:
  • Monthly HSD-LSM Divergence Dashboard — Mandatory OGRA/Petroleum Division public publication. Automatic enforcement trigger when formalization ratio falls below 90% for two consecutive months.
  • OMC Margin Deregulation — Move from fixed OMC (Rs 10.50/L) and dealer (Rs 12.50/L) margins to permitted bands (OGRA-notified maximum; OMCs and dealers compete below cap). Eliminates the fixed margin as a riskless informal-channel profit floor.
  • Supply-Chain Traceability — GPS-tagged tank lorry delivery confirmation, OMC product-source declaration per cargo, OGRA reconciliation of upliftment vs declared supply. Digital reconciliation for every litre claimed through the freight system. Pipeline-first condition: if pipeline capacity is available, recoverable road freight cannot exceed pipeline tariff + handling + efficient last-mile.
  • Border Formalization / Fuel Voucher Scheme — On-border purchase limits for Iranian fuel corridors; formalization voucher incentive for informal traders to transition to regulated supply chains.

The sequencing rule: Pillars 1 (WNSC formula) and 4 (PDL split) must be initiated in Phase 1 because they set the commercial logic for all subsequent steps. IFEM unbundling (Pillar 3) and margin deregulation (Pillar 6) are Phase 2–3 because they require the pricing foundation to be in place first. Strategic storage (Pillar 5) has a long build time and must start immediately regardless of other sequencing. G2G supply (Pillar 2) is diplomatically independent and should run in parallel from day one.
