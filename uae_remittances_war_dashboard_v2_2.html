Historical Overview

UAE: Pakistan's Largest Single Remittance Source

The UAE has been the dominant source of remittances to Pakistan since the mid-1970s when Pakistani labour migration to the Gulf began in earnest. Total UAE inflows have grown from under $200M per annum in the early 2000s to over $7.8 billion in FY25, representing ~20% of all remittances. The composition — dominated by Dubai, followed by Abu Dhabi — reflects the concentration of Pakistani expatriate labour in the commercial and services sectors.

Remittance flows exhibit persistent monthly seasonality driven by (i) seasonal employment patterns in the Gulf, (ii) Islamic festivals — particularly Ramazan, Eid ul Fitr, and Eid ul Azha — when families in Pakistan require additional cash, and (iii) calendar effects around Pakistani national holidays and school terms.

Model Documentation

Methodology: Seasonal Decomposition & Excess Estimation

Step 1 — Data Construction

Monthly country-wise workers' remittances from SBP's Home Remittances Archive were compiled across three sheets spanning 1972–May 2026. UAE data is available from July 1974. All values are in USD million on a cash-flow basis.

Step 2 — Long-Run Trend + Seasonal Regression

A log-linear OLS model was estimated on UAE monthly data from July 2010 to June 2025 (180 observations):

ln(UAE_t) = α + β·t + Σ γ_m · I(month=m) + ε_t

Where t is a linear time trend and I(month=m) are monthly dummy variables (January as baseline). This captures both the underlying growth trajectory and the average seasonal pattern simultaneously.

Step 3 — Seasonal Factor Extraction

Monthly seasonal factors are derived by exponentiating the estimated month-dummy coefficients and normalising so the average equals 1.0. These factors embody the average historical effect of each month — including embedded festival effects that historically fall in those months.

Step 4 — Baseline Projection

The model is extrapolated forward from June 2025 using the estimated trend rate (~9% per annum) and monthly seasonal factors. The baseline represents what UAE remittances would have been absent any shock, holding the 2010–2025 structural relationship intact.

Step 5 — Excess Computation

Excess = Actual − Baseline for each post-June 2025 month. War-period months (March–May 2026) are then further decomposed to isolate the Eid ul Azha festival premium in May 2026.

Step 6 — Robustness via Alternative Methods
  1. Pre-war FY26 run-rate: Uses the Jul 2025–Feb 2026 trend (+$30M/month) to project March–May counterfactual, capturing the FY26 structural shift
  2. YoY control group: Compares UAE YoY growth to the growth rate of all non-UAE Pakistan remittances as a benchmark
Step 7 — Occupation-Based Disaggregation

BEOE registered emigration data (1971–2026) provides annual flows by five occupational tiers: Highly Qualified, Highly Skilled, Skilled, Semi-Skilled, and Unskilled. The UAE diaspora stock is estimated at ~1.4M, with an occupation mix adjusted relative to national annual flows to reflect the higher tenure and professional density of the UAE community (see Occupation Profile tab). Monthly income, savings rates, and remittance propensities are calibrated from ILO Gulf wage surveys, World Bank KNOMAD data, and UAE Ministry of Labour benchmarks. The model implies $408M/month in theoretical salary-based remittance capacity — covering ~63% of the FY25 actual of $652M/month. The ~37% gap reflects business profit transfers, trade settlements, and property transactions concentrated in the HQ and HS tiers.

Model Caveats
The long-run model under-predicts FY25 by ~15–30% (Jan–Jun 2025 actuals exceed forecast), suggesting a genuine structural level-shift in UAE remittances predating the war. This structural shift — possibly driven by formalisation of hawala channels, SBP incentives, or genuine income growth — means the $600M excess estimate is an upper bound. The $500M war-adjusted figure and the $225M control-group estimate form lower and midpoint bounds.
Data Quality Note
April 2026 is marked as "Revised" (Apr-26R) in the source data, suggesting the figure may be subsequently updated. May 2026 data is the latest available (released June 2026) and may also be subject to minor revision. The core finding — that May 2026 UAE remittances at $1.007B are historically exceptional — is robust to any plausible revision.

Occupation-Level Analysis

Who Sends the Money? Occupation, Income & War-Period Attribution

Pakistan's Bureau of Emigration & Overseas Employment (BEOE) records every registered worker by occupational tier. Mapping those flows to income, savings rates, and remittance propensity — then estimating the UAE diaspora stock by tier — allows us to disaggregate the $500M war-period excess by worker category. The findings are striking: the Highly Qualified tier (≈8% of UAE-based Pakistanis) likely drove 35–40% of the war-shock surplus, primarily through precautionary asset repatriation and flight-capital transfers.

Structural Finding — 2025 Occupation Shift
In 2025, Unskilled workers reached 61% of all registered emigrants — the highest share on record — while Skilled (29%) and Semi-Skilled (5.5%) contracted sharply. This reverses the 2022–2023 pattern when Highly Skilled surged to 5.3% (45,687 workers) during the post-COVID Gulf recovery boom. The 2025 shift toward Unskilled likely reflects demand from large UAE/Saudi infrastructure projects (Dubai Expo legacy construction, NEOM-adjacent supply chain). Critically, this annual flow mix does not match the stock composition — the accumulated diaspora in UAE has a much higher share of established professionals.

The Concentration Finding
The Highly Qualified tier — just 8% of the diaspora — accounts for an estimated 36% of total worker-salary-based UAE remittances ($147M of $408M modelled monthly). Policy Implication
If the $500M war-driven surplus is predominantly a Highly Qualified "capital flight" phenomenon, then sustainability is limited: once accumulated UAE-side savings are repatriated, the excess normalises regardless of the conflict's duration. The Skilled tier's front-loading may also self-reverse within 2–4 months as workers exhaust their UAE savings buffer. This suggests May 2026 may represent a peak in war-driven flows, with a gradual moderation expected unless conflict escalates further.

Global Remittance Economy

Pakistan's Remittance Landscape: Where UAE Sits in the World

Pakistan's FY25 remittance receipts of $38.3 billion are the highest on record — a 26.7% jump over FY24. Thirteen countries individually contribute over $100M annually. The UAE is the second-largest source by absolute volume, but ranks only 8th by per-worker yield — reflecting the GCC's large, lower-wage contract labour base relative to Western diaspora. Understanding where UAE remittances sit in this global hierarchy is critical to contextualising the war-period shock.

Kuwait: The Professional Diaspora Signal
Kuwait's $13,154 per-worker yield — 3.8× Saudi Arabia and 2.7× UAE — is not a market anomaly. It signals the value of a high-tenure, professional-composition diaspora. Kuwait's ~65,000 active workers are almost entirely long-tenured professionals (doctors, engineers, business owners) who pre-date the 2006–2016 ban and were never in BEOE registers. They represent the closest analogue to what Pakistan's UAE professional community (HQ tier) looks like in yield terms — and illuminate why HQ workers drove the majority of the war-period excess despite being only 8% of the UAE diaspora.

Gulf = volume; Western = yield

UAE FY25 surge pre-dates war
UAE grew +41.5% in FY25 (FY24: $5.5B → FY25: $7.8B) before the March 2026 conflict		 The structural level-shift in UAE remittances is real and predates the war. Even without the war excess, UAE appears to be in a new structural range of $7–8B/year.
Kuwait's professional premium ($13,154/worker)
65K workers generating $855M = 3.8× Saudi yield per worker		 Confirms HQ/professional tier generates disproportionate remittance value — the same dynamic driving the war-period HQ attribution model for UAE.
Europe as an emerging high-yield corridor
UK+EU = $10.45B combined in FY25 — nearly equal to Saudi Arabia alone		 European corridor now structurally comparable to Saudi in aggregate. Per-worker yield (EU: $11,358) far exceeds GCC. Long-run diversification reduces geopolitical concentration risk.
UAE calibrated stock: 1.6M
BEOE flow model (686K) covers only 43% of actual UAE Pakistani stock		 Informal labour, undocumented workers, and long-tenure settled diaspora are a large share of UAE Pakistanis. The occupation model's 1.4M working assumption is a conservative mid-point.
FY26 war excess in macro terms
$500M over 3 months = 6.4% of UAE's annual base and 1.3% of national total		 While small as a share of Pakistan's total remittances, it represents a meaningful impulse for foreign exchange reserves. SBP received ~$167M/month in excess inflows above baseline during the conflict window.
