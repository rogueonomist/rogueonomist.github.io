Thar Coal Tariff Trajectory, 2019–2035

SECMC (Block-II) and SSRL (Block-I), rebuilt bottom-up: legacy debt runs its own amortization course, expansion capex is financed separately at a lower cost of capital, fixed costs are literally spread over the larger post-expansion tonnage, and EV/grid electrification is layered onto fuel and power. This view brings the composite tariff line back alongside the full component breakdown, so the headline trajectory and the mechanics behind it sit in one place. All post-2025 figures are indicative projections built on stated assumptions — they are not determined or filed tariffs.

Reading the six panels together
  • Fuel falls every year, continuously — EV/IPCC adoption compounding, not a one-time step. By 2035 it is down ~52% (SECMC) and ~59% (SSRL) from 2025.
  • Power falls even further in percentage terms (~54% SECMC, ~60% SSRL) as grid electrification phases out diesel gensets at dewatering — but it was a small line item, so its dollar contribution to the total decline is modest.
  • Fixed O&M and ROE+ROEDC are flat, step down once at expansion, then flat again — these are literally being spread over the larger tonnage; no further gradual improvement without another expansion.
  • Debt + Interest is the busiest line: a dilution step down, a small offsetting bump from the new expansion tranche's own service, then a second drop once legacy debt fully retires.

How to read this — for each mine, the total 2025→2035 decline is decomposed in sequence: (i) what the tariff would do on the existing determination alone, no new levers (pure legacy debt/ROE amortization); (ii) how much further it falls once EV adoption and grid electrification are layered on, holding capacity fixed; (iii) how much further once the capacity expansion — with diluted fixed costs and cheaper new financing — is added on top. For both mines, legacy amortization is still the single largest contributor (~62–64% of the total decline). Scale-up is the second-largest lever (~22–26%); electrification, while continuous, is the smallest share in dollar terms (~10–16%) because fuel and power were never the largest line items.
