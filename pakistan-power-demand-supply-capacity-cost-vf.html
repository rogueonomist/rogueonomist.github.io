Solar methodology

Core distinction: imported solar panels create a gross technical generation potential, but only a portion appears as reduced grid withdrawals.

Calibrated BTM gross generation TWh = Calibrated BTM GW × Solar capacity factor × 8.76
Direct grid-displacing BTM TWh = sectoral BTM displacement path × BTM solar displacement level

The Solar tab therefore shows both the gross capacity/generation story and the grid-demand displacement story.

Peak demand and dependable capacity requirement

The table uses the dynamically computed post-BTM-and-battery peak. Country peak applies a 1.13× KE gross-up; reserve margin is applied to country peak.

Stylized 2035 night-time dependable capacity stack

Installed capacity is not the same as dependable peak-hour capacity. Solar is assumed to contribute zero at the summer midnight peak; wind is derated; hydel contribution is seasonal.

Interpretation for system planning

Planning implication: BTM solar primarily reduces energy and daytime load. The binding capacity requirement remains summer late-night / midnight cooling demand, which must be met by dependable hydro, thermal, nuclear, imports, wind coincidence, demand response, and battery discharge.
Operational implication: More BTM solar can make the daytime trough deeper without reducing the midnight peak. More batteries improve the peak, but four-hour storage is not a full substitute for firm capacity if the cooling plateau lasts longer than the discharge window.

Capacity-cost methodology

1. Fixed-cost pool identity

Fixed cost pool = Existing fleet CPP + annualized new IGCEP generation + annualized TSEP transmission

Existing CPP is based on the capacity-cost model path from the workbook Summary sheet, which already incorporates PPA/debt runoff dynamics from the Projection Model and Debt Maturity Tracker. New IGCEP generation and TSEP are annualized using the selected WACC and scaled by FX, inflation and capex stress assumptions.

2. Per-kWh identity

Capacity cost PKR/kWh = fixed cost pool PKR bn × 1,000 ÷ recoverable grid GWh

Unit conversion is explicit: 1 PKR bn / 1 GWh = PKR 1,000/kWh. Therefore the model multiplies the PKR bn fixed-cost pool by 1,000 and divides by the live grid-demand GWh denominator from the demand model. This avoids the prior under-statement error.

Formula audit: the capacity-cost line is now checked against the identity PKR/kWh = PKR billion × 1,000 ÷ GWh. For example, if the fixed-cost pool is PKR 4,463 bn and live grid demand is 140,025 GWh, the resulting capacity charge is about PKR 31.87/kWh. If the denominator is 149,830 GWh, the same pool is PKR 29.79/kWh.
Debt-runoff audit: existing-capacity totals are taken from the workbook Summary path, while the component split now uses the year-wise Component Breakdown path instead of applying a flat FY2024-25 debt share to all years. This prevents repaid debt from being mechanically carried forward in the principal and interest component rows.

Tariff methodology

Final tariff = (capacity cost + variable cost + distribution margin) ÷ (1 - loss rate)
Final US cents/kWh = final PKR/kWh ÷ PKR/USD × 100

Variable cost and distribution margin are deliberately kept as transparent model assumptions. Capacity cost is calculated from the fixed-cost pool and live grid units using the audited PKR bn × 1,000 ÷ GWh conversion.

Model structure and audit notes

1. Demand identity

True electricity demand = Grid demand + Direct BTM solar self-consumption + Battery-enabled solar shifting

Grid demand is what must be served by the grid after BTM solar and battery effects. True demand is the underlying electricity service demand before these behind-the-meter reductions.

2. Solar identity

Average MW = Annual GWh ÷ 8.76
8-hour solar-window MW = Annual GWh ÷ 2.92

Solar is shown in both annual GWh and daylight-window MW to avoid understating the intraday effect.

3. Battery identity

4-hour equivalent GW = usable solar-linked battery GWh ÷ 4

Batteries are most useful in MW terms around the post-sunset window, not as a flat annual average.

4. Cooling identity

Cooling is treated as a structural driver of underlying demand and peak. BTM solar reduces daytime grid cooling demand, while batteries can move part of solar output into evening demand windows.
