Section 1 · Demand
Structural decline with FY2024-25 rebound — now overshadowed by the Apr 2026 price shock
Pakistan's kerosene market consumed 65,964 tonnes in FY2024-25 (+15.85% YoY rebound), driven by a Rs 28/L YoY price decline. The five-year ACGR of −5.8% confirms structural displacement by rural electrification and LPG. FY2025-26 consumption data is not yet published, but the extraordinary Apr 2026 price shock — kerosene reaching Rs 467.48/L at its peak — will almost certainly cause severe demand destruction in Q4 FY2025-26 and into FY2026-27 as rural households accelerate substitution to LPG, firewood, and solar lanterns.

Structural break, not a cycle: At Rs 450/L, kerosene is no longer the cheap rural fuel. It now trades at a Rs 57/L premium over petrol (Apr 25, 2026: petrol Rs 393.35 vs kerosene Rs 450.15). Rural households who chose kerosene over LPG on price grounds now face the opposite calculus. This shock may permanently accelerate substitution — collapsing the market to a 30,000–40,000 T structural floor over 2–3 years even if Brent retreats.

Section 2 · Pricing
Three price regimes: FY2023-24 spike, FY2024-25 normalisation, FY2025-26 Hormuz shock
FY2023-24 saw kerosene peak at Rs 244.81/L (Sep 2023) before a long retreat. FY2024-25 corrected to an average of ~Rs 167/L — enabling the demand rebound. FY2025-26 began benignly near Rs 169/L and drifted to ~Rs 205/L by December 2025, then fell back to Rs 176.81/L in early March 2026. The Feb 28 Iran strikes then triggered a once-in-a-generation shock: kerosene surged to Rs 467.48/L in 33 days. PM intervention on Apr 11 brought it to Rs 450.15/L, where it has held.

The Hormuz Shock in numbers: Kerosene went from Rs 176.81/L (Mar 1, 2026) to Rs 467.48/L (Apr 3) — a Rs 290.67 surge (+164%) in 33 days. For a rural household consuming ~20 litres/month, monthly kerosene spending jumped from ~Rs 3,536 to ~Rs 9,350. The current price (Rs 450.15/L) is still +166% year-on-year versus April 2025's Rs 169.38/L — and Rs 57/L above petrol (Rs 393.35). This is the most severe kerosene price shock in Pakistan's recorded history.

Section 3 · Mechanism
Price construction: ex-refinery dominates — levies held flat during the crisis
The fixed sale price of kerosene is: ex-refinery price + Customs/Excise Duty + PDL + OMC margin. Kerosene carries zero GST, zero dealer commission, and a PDL of only Rs 1.58/L. Customs Duty was raised to Rs 18.95/L in April 2025. During the Mar–Apr 2026 crisis, the government held levies flat — meaning nearly 100% of the shock was ex-refinery pass-through. At Rs 450.15/L, approximately Rs 420/L is ex-refinery component alone.

Price inversion (Apr 2026): In pre-crisis terms, kerosene carried an Rs 88.47/L levy advantage over petrol (worth ~PKR 7 billion/year in foregone revenue). In April 2026, ex-refinery prices drove kerosene to Rs 450.15/L while petrol sat at Rs 393.35/L — kerosene is now Rs 57/L more expensive than petrol. The social concession has been fully eroded by market forces alone, without any further government levy action.

Section 4 · Supply
Domestic supply remains intact — Hormuz crisis hit prices, not physical availability
All of Pakistan's kerosene consumption is domestically produced. The Hormuz Strait crisis of Feb–Apr 2026 threatened oil supply chains but Pakistan held adequate stocks (reported at 27 days of petrol reserves in March 2026). Kerosene, being a domestic refinery by-product of crude already onshore, faced no physical shortage — the price spike was purely a crude-cost pass-through. As demand collapses at Rs 450+/L, refineries will accumulate surplus kerosene cuts — a mild structural oversupply problem.

Surplus dynamic: As demand collapses under Rs 450+/L prices, refineries producing kerosene as a by-product face a disposal problem. Surplus will push ex-refinery prices lower even before Brent retreats — creating a partial self-correcting mechanism. This is why kerosene prices may normalise faster than petrol once the crisis recedes.

Section 5 · Market Context & Outlook
From social fuel to unaffordable premium — the crisis inverts kerosene's market position
Before the Hormuz crisis, kerosene was Pakistan's most concessionally priced fuel — Rs 89/L cheaper than petrol in June 2025. By April 2026, that relationship had inverted: kerosene at Rs 450.15/L is Rs 57 more expensive than petrol (Rs 393.35/L). This eliminates the fundamental economic rationale for kerosene use. FY2024-25 remains the last confirmed consumption year at 65,964 T — the crisis will have drastically altered this picture when FY2025-26 data is published.

Policy prescription (updated): The Hormuz shock has done what years of subsidy reform could not — made kerosene unaffordable relative to substitutes. The government should use this window to formally restructure kerosene pricing: introduce a modest GST (5%), raise PDL progressively to Rs 15–20/L, and redirect savings to targeted LPG subsidies or solar lantern programmes for off-grid households. Returning to pre-crisis concessional pricing would be fiscally wasteful and structurally counterproductive.
