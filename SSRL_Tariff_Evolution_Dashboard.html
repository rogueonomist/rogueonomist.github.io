Section A

Executive Snapshot

SSRL's tariff history is, at its core, a regulatory discipline story. Unlike SECMC which extracted savings through multi-phase scale-up, SSRL has operated at a single capacity of 7.8 MTPA since inception. The most powerful tariff lever deployed so far has been TCEB's consistent rejection of inflated petitions. Scale-up remains the primary untapped lever: every MTPA added to Block-I will dilute the fixed capacity burden and bend the cost curve downward in ways that regulatory discipline alone cannot achieve.

The SSRL Tariff Story
Why this mine's tariff history is structurally different from SECMC
SSRL has not yet scaled. The entire tariff record — all seven regulatory milestones from 2016 to 2025 — covers the same 7.8 MTPA mine. This means Block-I has not yet unlocked the most powerful tariff lever available to it: fixed cost dilution through expanded production volume. Every reduction achieved so far has come from regulatory discipline, cost audit and component rationalisation, not scale.
Regulatory DisciplineTCEB/TCTDC consistently cut inflated capex, ROEDC, financing charges, and non-EPC costs. The FC petition of $56.75/ton was determined at $37.36/ton — a 34% reduction.
Untapped ScaleBlock-I sits on the same geological platform as SECMC's multi-MTPA expansion. A scale-up to 12–18 MTPA would dilute fixed costs, ROE/ROEDC, insurance and O&M per tonne by 25–45%.
Fuel/Diesel FrontierFuel cost now represents ~35–40% of the production payment. At current diesel prices, this is the single largest avoidable variable cost and is not addressed by scale alone.
Electrification OpportunityThe FC Review (Aug 2023) explicitly directed SSRL to transition to electric vehicles and mine-mouth power. This is now an embedded regulatory expectation, not a voluntary option.

Section B

Tariff Evolution: What Moved and Why

The headline pattern is not simple decline. SSRL's tariff moved up and down across milestones — rising at FS Review, dropping sharply at CS, rising again at FC/FC Review, and stabilising at COD. The underlying mechanics are more instructive than the headline numbers: capacity payment compression came from regulatory discipline, while production payment has drifted upward with real-world diesel, fleet and operational costs.

Section C

Regulatory Discipline: The Dominant Tariff Lever (So Far)

In the absence of scale-up, TCEB's regulatory scrutiny has been the primary cost-compression mechanism for SSRL. Every major petition was cut materially — total regulatory savings across all petitions exceed USD 25/tonne. The most aggressive example: SSRL's 2023 FC petition of $56.75/ton was reduced to $37.36/ton, a 34% cut driven by capex audit, machinery assessment, financing discipline and operational benchmarking.

Section D

Component Decomposition: What's Moving and What Isn't

Breaking the COD tariff into its constituent parts reveals the next efficiency frontier. Fuel/diesel dominates the production payment; fixed capacity recovery (ROE, fixed O&M, debt service) dominates the capacity payment. Scale-up addresses capacity components; electrification and fuel benchmarking address production components.

Section F

Scale-Up & Electrification Roadmap

SSRL's next tariff frontier requires a two-track programme: (1) Mine scale-up from 7.8 MTPA toward 12–18 MTPA to dilute fixed capital costs — the lever SECMC has already proven works; and (2) Production cost discipline through fuel benchmarking, electrification, mine-mouth power and localization. Scale without operational discipline delivers only partial savings; operational discipline without scale leaves capacity costs unaddressed.

1. Block-I Scale-Up
Capacity payment reduction
  • 7.8 → 10 → 14 → 18 MTPA expansion path, leveraging existing pit, roads, dewatering and CHS.
  • Benchmark incremental capex per MTPA — target <USD 50M/MTPA.
  • Require declining tariff glidepath as condition of expansion approval.
  • Sub-account discipline to ring-fence scale benefits for consumers.
2. Fuel Benchmarking
Production payment control
  • Adopt formal litres/BCM target — converging toward ≤0.85 L/BCM within 3 years.
  • Mandate GPS, engine telematics, payload monitoring and haul-road condition reporting.
  • Benchmarking as a COD true-up condition — quarterly fuel intensity reporting.
  • Idle-time reduction programme and driver behaviour monitoring.
3. Power & Static Load Electrification
Near-term diesel displacement
  • Grid / mine-mouth power for dewatering, CHS, workshops and MSF (mandated in FC Review).
  • Solar BOOT for critical daytime loads — 5–20 MW phased installation.
  • VFD motors for dewatering pumps and conveyor systems.
  • Certified kWh cost audited quarterly; power savings passed to TCPBA.
4. Mobile Fleet EV Transition
Structural OPEX reduction
  • EV dump truck and trolley-assist pilots endorsed in FC Review Aug 2023 order.
  • IPCC/semi-mobile crusher feasibility as Block-I deepens (haul distance increases).
  • Localization plan for EV components, charging infrastructure and battery maintenance.
  • Tariff recognition only after life-cycle cost verification and COD audit.

Annex B — Important Model Cautions
Scope and Limitations
Not a DeterminationThe dynamic model is an indicative planning tool. It does not replace TCEB tariff determination, COD true-up, MYT reconciliation, or any formal regulatory process.
Stage DifferencesThe seven milestones use different FX, diesel price, CPI, KIBOR/LIBOR and mine-plan assumptions. They should be read as directional evidence, not a constant-assumption time series.
Production Payment RiseUnlike SECMC, SSRL's production payment has drifted UP between stages (real diesel, fleet and OpEx inflation). Scale alone does not solve this — operational efficiency is a separate programme.
Electrification CreditEV/IPCC benefits are partially credited. Full credit requires capex verification, charging infrastructure, battery replacement schedules, availability guarantees, and life-cycle cost validation.
Regulatory ContextScale-up tariffs will be independently determined by TCEB at each stage. The model benchmarks against demonstrated patterns — actual outcomes depend on project execution, financing structure and regulatory process.
