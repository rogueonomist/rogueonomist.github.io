Weekly price level
National and city price trends

National average price rose from roughly PKR 860 per 20kg in September 2019 to over PKR 2,800 at the August 2023 peak — a period that includes Pakistan's 2023 wheat/flour supply crisis — before retreating through 2024–25 and climbing again into 2026. Karachi (a port/import hub) tends to lead and smooth national moves; inland mill markets like Lahore and Multan are noisier and lag.

The Rabi wheat crop is sown October–December and harvested April–May, so the market is flush with new grain through early summer (prices trough in August, about 5.9% below trend) and stocks deplete by autumn just as the next crop is being sown (prices peak in September, about 8.4% above trend).

The harvest-vs-lean split is statistically significant (Welch t-test p=0.004; Mann-Whitney p<0.0001), but seasonality is a secondary driver — STL seasonal strength is only 0.016, meaning trend and shocks (like the 2023 crisis) explain far more of the variance than the calendar does.

Long-run output growth has come almost entirely from yield, not area: production grew at a 2.23%/yr CAGR and yield at 1.67%/yr, while cultivated area expanded just 0.55%/yr. The yield trend is unusually steady (R²=0.95 against a simple time trend), but year-to-year production and yield growth still carries meaningful noise (~7–8% standard deviation) from weather and input-cost shocks.

Production vs. price
Only four crop years (2019-20 to 2022-23) overlap with the price series — too few for a robust test — but the available data shows no reliable negative relationship between domestic production growth and price growth (correlation +0.31, not significant, n=3). In 2022-23 production rose 7.4% while average price still rose 54%, consistent with the price series being dominated by currency, inflation and policy shocks rather than the size of the domestic harvest.
