Section 1
The demand decomposition identity
The core equation: Historically, Total Electricity Demand (TED) ≈ Grid Demand because the grid was the only source of electricity. With the rapid rise of behind-the-meter (BTM) solar and batteries, the equation has fractured into three components. TED still grows with GDP, but the grid captures a shrinking share of that growth. This model tracks all three layers simultaneously, anchored to FY2025 actuals.

Key observation: Grid generation peaked at 154,000 GWh in FY2022 and has since declined to 140,420 GWh in FY2025 despite 3% GDP growth — a 9% absolute fall. Over the same period, BTM solar stock grew from ~5 GW to 26.8 GW, absorbing an estimated 24 TWh of incremental demand that would otherwise have come from the grid. Without this BTM wedge, grid generation would have exceeded 165,000 GWh by FY2025.

Scenario comparison — FY2035 grid retail demand (TWh)
Exhibit 9 — FY2035 grid demand under four scenarios
🌱 Low growth scenario
GDP 2.5%, elasticity 0.9×, high solar additions (5GW/yr), fast battery (400 cycles). Grid demand grows slowest — barely 1.5% CAGR. Grid share of TED falls to ~65% by FY2035.
⚖ Base scenario
GDP 3.5%, elasticity 1.1×, moderate solar additions (4GW declining), base batteries (300 cycles). Grid share of TED falls from 79% to ~70% by FY2035. Broadly consistent with IGCEP Low BAU.
📈 High growth scenario
GDP 5%, elasticity 1.2×, slower solar (2.5GW/yr), shallow batteries (200 cycles). Grid demand CAGR 4.2% — approaches IGCEP High case. Grid share remains ~77% as TED grows faster than BTM additions.
Methodology notes
This model operates in retail TWh throughout. T&D losses (assumed 20%, per NEPRA SoI FY2024 actual 19.8%) are applied when converting to generation-gross equivalents for IGCEP cross-checks only. The model is anchored to three independently verified FY2025 data points: grid retail sales 113.1 TWh (EYB 2024-25), BTM solar stock 26.8 GW and self-consumed energy 30.4 TWh (Dec 2025 BTM Solar Revised Memo). Battery projections are sourced directly from Solar_Import_Projections.xlsx (Bloomberg NEF–calibrated). The day/evening split (41%/59%) is computed from 8,760 ISMO hourly demand observations for FY2025. GDP growth assumption of 3.5% is the IGCEP Low BAU midpoint (3.52% CAGR). All estimates distinguished from confirmed data.
