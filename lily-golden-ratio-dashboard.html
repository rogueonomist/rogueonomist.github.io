The Lily Golden Ratio

A measured design specification for placing a stargazer lily on a buttercream tier — derived from ten approved reference cakes and a single governing equation that ties span, angle, and rise together.

Core relationship: L · sin θ ≈ D — lily span, times the sine of its face angle, equals the cake diameter.

The Correction
Where the golden section actually lives

Lily span to cake diameter is not φ (1.618) — it's ≈1.05–1.10:1. The golden section instead governs the elevation silhouette: cake body occupies 0.618 of the total height, the flower supplies the minor 0.382. Since R = (L⁄2)·sin θ, forcing R = 0.494D collapses span, angle, and rise into one equation.

L · sin θ ≈ D
Span × sine(face angle) = cake diameter — the floor is L ≥ D, since sin θ cannot exceed 1.

Detail
Overhang is two numbers, not one

Conflating plan overhang with the "one-petal rule" is where builds go wrong. The petal that visually breaks the front rim barely projects past the cake's centre in plan — the composition looks cantilevered but the centre of mass stays inside the footprint.

Plan overhang
≈ 0.15–0.18 D

On the side the throat is offset toward. O = L⁄2 + e − D⁄2, reduced by tilt foreshortening.

Elevation overhang (one-petal rule)
≈ 0.03 D

The front-rim petal's true plan reach — it sits over the centre, but drops below the top-edge sightline, so it reads as overhanging.

Field checks
01
Lay-across test
Rest the lily flat across the cake top — it should just overhang both rims by a fingertip's width.
02
Two-thirds test
In elevation the cake body should read as roughly two-thirds of total height, flower one-third.
03
Plumb-line test
Drop an imaginary vertical from the outermost petal tip — it should land on cake or board, never past it.
04
Void test
Find the emptiest quadrant on the top surface. More than a quarter empty means filler is missing.
Verification protocol: current figures come from screenshots with real perspective distortion (±10%). Photograph square-on at cake-top height with a scale reference on the board, one plan view and one elevation per cake, to tighten this to ±3% — and to settle whether θ genuinely tracks the L·sinθ ≈ D curve or the baker holds θ fixed and lets R float.
