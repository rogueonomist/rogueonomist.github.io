Global Crude & Products
How oil prices evolved through the Hormuz crisis
The closure of the Strait of Hormuz — through which ~20 mb/d of crude, condensate, and refined products normally transit — created the largest supply disruption in oil market history. Prior to the conflict, global markets were oversupplied and prices were falling. The shock reversed this entirely: Brent futures soared from $72/bbl before the war to nearly $128/bbl at the April 2 peak, while physical crude benchmarks disconnected sharply from futures, with Dubai crude touching $166/bbl and North Sea Dated crossing $130/bbl. Product markets were even more severely disrupted, with middle distillate cracks reaching all-time highs.

Supply disruption scale: The IEA estimates Gulf crude production fell by at least 10 mb/d by mid-March, with overall global supply disrupted by 7.5–9.1 mb/d at its peak. Alternative export routes (Yanbu on the Red Sea, Ceyhan pipeline, Fujairah) had capacity of only ~7.2 mb/d versus the 20 mb/d normally transiting Hormuz. Pakistan formally requested Saudi Arabia reroute deliveries via Yanbu, securing at least one bypass shipment.

Retail Fuel Prices
Pump prices — Pakistan weekly timeline & regional peer comparison
Pakistan's pump prices are determined fortnightly (shifted to weekly during the crisis) by OGRA using an Import Parity Price formula referenced to Arab Gulf Platts indices. The exchange rate (PKR/USD ~278–285 throughout) and the level of the Petroleum Development Levy are the two policy levers. The regional picture is striking: countries with heavy fuel subsidies (Malaysia, Indonesia, India) absorbed much of the shock domestically, while price-takers like Pakistan and Sri Lanka passed on substantially more to consumers.

India's policy contrast: While Pakistan faces the highest absolute price increase among South Asian peers, India effectively insulated consumers by cutting excise duty on petrol and diesel by Rs. 10/L on March 27, 2026, while simultaneously sourcing replacement crude from Russia at discounted prices. India's strategic reserve of 90+ days and its ability to rapidly pivot to Russian supply were critical buffers — advantages Pakistan did not have.
Price Level Impact
Inflation transmission — how the fuel shock spread through Pakistan's economy
Pakistan's consumer price index returned to double digits (10.9% YoY) in April 2026 — for the first time in 21 months — driven by the cascading effect of the fuel price shock. The transmission works across multiple channels: direct transport costs, agricultural production costs, manufacturing inputs, power generation, and second-round wage effects. Diesel is the economy's central nervous system: it powers freight trucks (90%+ of cargo), agricultural machinery, industrial captive generation, and water pumps. The weekly Sensitive Price Indicator (SPI) published by PBS captures real-time price changes in 51 essential items across 17 urban centres.

Fiscal Analysis
Pakistan pump price composition — how much is tax?
Pakistan's petrol pump price is set by OGRA using an Import Parity Price (IPP) formula. The final price is the sum of six components: the ex-refinery/import cost (the international crude benchmark converted at the USD/PKR rate), port and import charges, the Inland Freight Equalisation Margin (IFEM), Oil Marketing Company (OMC) margin, dealer commission, the Petroleum Development Levy (PDL), and General Sales Tax. Under Pakistan's IMF program, the government cannot permanently waive petroleum taxes — creating a fiscal bind when oil prices surge. The April 3, 2026 hike involved both a full pass-through of elevated import costs AND a deliberate increase in the PDL from ~Rs. 78/L to Rs. 161/L to meet IMF revenue targets. The PM then temporarily cut PDL by Rs. 80/L following public outrage.

Government Tax Component — Evolution Through the Crisis
PDL is a fixed per-litre charge regardless of crude price. GST is applied as a percentage. Both components create a "tax floor" that keeps pump prices elevated even when crude prices fall.

Key policy insight — the tax floor problem: Of the Rs. 401.81 pump price on May 7, at least Rs. 149 (37%) is government taxes and levies. The Petroleum Development Levy of Rs. 78/L is a FIXED amount — it does not fall when crude prices fall. This creates a structural "tax floor" that prevents pump prices from declining proportionally to international crude. When crude was at its pre-war level of ~$72/bbl, the PDL of Rs. 78 was ~30% of the pump price. If crude returns to $72/bbl, the PDL would be ~38% of a theoretically lower pump price — meaning Pakistanis would not see full relief. The IMF program requires maintaining PDL revenue at ~Rs. 1,070 billion/year, leaving limited room to reduce the per-litre charge.
Diesel (HSD) Tax Note

In the April 3 hike, the government abolished the Petroleum Development Levy on Diesel (HSD) entirely — while simultaneously raising it to Rs. 161/L on petrol — in response to the extreme economic sensitivity of diesel for freight and agriculture. However, diesel pump prices still hit Rs. 520/L due to the surge in the underlying import cost (at $128/bbl crude). By May 7, with GST re-applied, diesel (Rs. 402/L) carries roughly Rs. 85-90/L in taxes (~21% of pump price).

IMF Program Constraint

Pakistan's 2023 Extended Fund Facility with the IMF prohibits permanent fuel subsidies. The government must collect at least Rs. 1,070 billion/year in petroleum levies. This forced the April 3 PDL hike to Rs. 161/L — and means even the "temporary" Rs. 80 PDL cut must be reversed. Pakistan's import bill surged from $300M/week pre-war to $800M/week, erasing two years of fiscal consolidation gains in weeks.

Motorcycle Subsidy Scheme

Following the April 3 shock, the PM introduced a Rs. 100/L motorcycle petrol subsidy (up to 20 litres per fill for 3 months) delivered via CNIC/biometric verification at designated pumps. This reduces the effective price for ~18 million motorcycle owners from Rs. 393/L to Rs. 293/L — providing targeted relief to lower-income daily commuters who depend on two-wheelers. Punjab and Sindh registration was active as of late April.
