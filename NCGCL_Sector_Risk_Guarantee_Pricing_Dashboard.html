What the data shows

Sector NPL ratio more than halved on a weighted basis — from 15.3% in 2011 to 7.7% in 2018 — as gross advances quadrupled from PKR 3.78 trillion past PKR 8.7 trillion. The 2019–2020 COVID-adjacent window pushed the ratio back up to 8.9%, after which a sustained decline brought it to a fifteen-year low of 6.93% by 2025 — even as the absolute NPL stock nearly doubled to PKR 1.01 trillion (from PKR 578 billion in 2011), diluted by a much larger PKR 14.6 trillion lending base.

Bank vs. MFB divergence

Microfinance Banks carried a near-identical NPL ratio to commercial Banks in 2011 (15.6% vs. 17.1% simple average) but de-risked faster on a much smaller, faster-growing base — reaching 6.9% by 2025 against 13.0% for Banks on a simple-average basis. The structural quality gap that this implies for first-loss tranche pricing is addressed in Section 03.

PIT PD is not a static credit-policy assumption — it moves with the cycle, from 5.6% sector-wide in 2011 to a low of 2.9% in 2017, and back up through COVID before settling at 3.85% in 2025. 04 · From Guarantee Fee to Lending Rate

Translating the Guarantee Fee Into a Credit Spread

A guarantee only covers a tranche of the underlying portfolio — the fee a PFI can reasonably pass through as an add-on to its lending rate is therefore the tranche fee rate scaled down by the guaranteed share of the portfolio, not the tranche rate itself.

Portfolio-equivalent spread (bps) = Tranche Full Price Rate × (Guaranteed Exposure ÷ Total Portfolio)

SBP’s zero-fee, 15%-cover guarantee on a PKR 300Bn book carries an implied annual credit subsidy of PKR 7.9Bn — roughly 14× the pricing gap on a illustrative NCGCL book that is fifteen times smaller, priced with a real fee, and structurally diversified across First Loss, Second Loss and Pari Passu. NCGCL recovers 11% of the fair commercial price on this scenario; SBP’s scheme recovers none of it — an unbudgeted, unpriced, and unrecorded contingent liability sitting on the public balance sheet at the exact moment of macro stress when it will be called.
07 · Recommendations

Credit Risk Is Mispriced in Pakistan. NCGCL May Fix Try To It.

The fifteen-year sector record is consistent and the pricing mathematics is not in dispute: the question is who absorbs the gap between the fair price of credit risk and the price a borrower or PFI is asked to pay for it.

Impact

SBP-administered zero-fee guarantee schemes are not free risk-sharing — they are a PKR 7.9Bn-a-year unfunded credit subsidy on a single PKR 300Bn book alone, embedded in the financial system without budget allocation, ECL provisioning, or capital backing. This distorts the market for risk-sharing instruments: PFIs and borrowers anchor on a zero price that has no commercial equivalent, crowding out fairly-priced private guarantee capacity and concealing the true fiscal cost of public credit support.

Action

NCGCL will price every guarantee to cover both Expected Credit Loss and the capital charge required to hold the risk — not administrative directive, not a politically convenient round number. Where affordability requires a lower market-facing fee, the gap will be closed with disclosed, sized, donor-sourced concessional capital — GAFSP-style blended finance — not by NCGCL absorbing the shortfall on its own balance sheet, and not by leaving it unpriced altogether.
