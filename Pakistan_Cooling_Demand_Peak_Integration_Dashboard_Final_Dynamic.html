Top-Down Calibration
Reconciling Appliance Stock with the FY2025 Grid Thermal Signature
The top-down method estimates cooling demand as the increment above February's average demand, the lowest monthly average in the ISMO dataset. This is a system-planning control total, not a claim that all incremental load is residential AC. The bottom-up model is calibrated through appliance stock, coincidence, grid loss gross-up and residual temperature-sensitive demand.

Hourly Anatomy
Cooling Peaks at Midnight, Not Noon
The FY2025 hourly profile shows the thermal increment is highest late night / early morning. This is the central reason the model treats BTM solar as a daytime hidden-load correction, not a reduction to the midnight peak cooling control total.

Model Architecture
1. PBS QIM + HS8 imports + legacy stock
2. Calibrate FY2025 to ISMO control total
3. AC adoption responds to GDP/income × elasticity
4. Weibull cohorts calculate replacement demand
5. Fan/AC stock converts into peak and energy
6. Residual block captures non-household thermal load
7. KE added separately; BTM handled hourly
