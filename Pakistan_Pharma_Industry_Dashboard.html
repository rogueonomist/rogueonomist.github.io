Key Finding
Pakistan's pharmaceutical market crossed Rs. 1,225 billion in Moving Annual Total sales as of April 2026, growing at 14.9% — a deceleration from 19.7% the prior year but still well above nominal GDP. Critically, the growth quality is improving: price contribution declined from 71% to 64% while volume contribution rose from 15% to 20%, signalling that market recovery is increasingly demand-driven rather than purely inflationary. New product launches now contribute 10.5% of growth, up from 5%, suggesting a pipeline revival after the 2022–24 shortage cycle.

Product Market Structure: Pakistan's pharmaceutical market is highly fragmented at the product level. Even the #1 product (Panadol) commands only 1.54% share. The top 200 products collectively cover roughly 92% of the market tracked. This fragmentation reflects the generic-dominated nature of the market — thousands of formulations, multiple manufacturers per molecule, and aggressive price competition at the generic tier. The branded originator segment (e.g., Augmentin, Brufen, Flagyl) remains the most revenue-intensive but faces increasing generic pressure.
What Is Driving Growth?
Central Finding
Pakistan's pharmaceutical growth is undergoing a structural transition: from crisis-price-inflation (2022–25) to volume-and-innovation-led expansion (2025–26). Price contribution to industry growth declined from 71% to 64% in one year. Volume contribution rose from 15% to 20%. New product launches nearly doubled their growth share (5% → 10.5%). This transition is meaningful — it implies that companies growing fastest in the new period are those capturing genuine demand, not merely riding price corrections.

Implication for GST Reform: The declining concentration means that a tiered GST structure would spread its incidence more broadly than previously. The structural shift toward mid-tier domestic companies strengthens the viability case for GST reform.

Policy Conclusion
The Rs. 375 billion pharmaceutical GST concession is the largest single tax expenditure in Pakistan's federal system — 20.6× the health development budget — yet it is structured with zero conditionality on pricing, access, or patient outcomes. It is not a health subsidy; it is a margin protector. The market data confirms this: Pakistan's pharmaceutical companies are posting 14–20% CAGRs. Getz Pharma, the market leader, generated Rs. 82 billion in annual revenue, growing at 14.7%. A 5% GST on Tier 2 medicines, paired with automatic reference pricing adjustment rights, is not an existential threat to an industry with 15–35% gross margins. It is a reallocation of Rs. 137 billion annually from an untargeted blanket concession to recoverable fiscal space that can fund genuine access interventions.

Policy Conclusion — India Benchmark
Pakistan's pharmaceutical prices are 60–1,090% above India's NPPA-regulated ceiling prices when converted to PKR. Against India's branded retail (the more comparable metric), Pakistan is at rough parity or modest premium (+9% median). The critical policy insight is that India's DPCO system — which covers 907 essential molecules, automatically adjusts prices via WPI, and is administered digitally through the IPDMS portal — has achieved genuine price discipline. Pakistan's DRAP cost-plus regime, by contrast, allows each price increase to be administered on application, with 12-24 month lags, and has no automatic pass-through mechanism. The proposed shift to reference pricing (with India at 40% weight in the basket) would bring Pakistan's ceiling prices meaningfully closer to India's levels for the overlapping molecules — generating both fiscal savings on the GST concession and tangible affordability gains for patients.
