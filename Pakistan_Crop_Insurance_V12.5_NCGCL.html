02 · Architecture

Five prices and cash flows that must not be collapsed into one number

V12.5 separates actuarial risk, commercial economics, affordability and post-loss liquidity. This avoids hidden cross-subsidies and prevents the renewal facility from contaminating the insurance premium.

Why rebasing matters

A district rate is not allowed to drift the national crop budget. After districts are scored and grouped, V12.5 rescales all district rates by one crop-specific factor so the eligible-area-weighted average returns to the approved crop anchor.

District rateᵢ = Risk scoreᵢ × [Crop anchor ÷ weighted mean risk score]   →   Tier rate = Σ(rateᵢ × eligible areaᵢ) ÷ Σ eligible areaᵢ
04 · Actuarial engine

From observed yield to a credible, tail-aware district risk score

The engine begins with an area-yield shortfall, converts observed payouts into loss cost, blends district and crop evidence through credibility, adds a controlled view of the upper tail, and only then assigns tiers.

01

Reference yield

Lock district × crop yield history and quality controls.

02

Threshold yield

Apply 80% coverage to the reference yield.

03

AYII loss

Measure proportional yield shortfall, capped at 100% SI.

04

Pure loss cost

Average historical indemnity over eligible seasons.

05

Central rate

Divide by 65% target loss ratio and add 1.25% evidence allowance.

06

Credibility

Blend district evidence with crop/module evidence; maize factor 55%.

07

Tail blend

Weight credible central evidence 75% and upper-tail evidence 25%.

08

Rebase

Preserve the crop anchor under eligible-area weights.

Assign tercile, apply controls, fix rate before sowing.

06 · Renewal liquidity

A claim repairs income loss; the RLF repairs only the remaining sowing cash gap

The model explicitly recognises that a stressed farmer still sells the surviving crop. Net retained sales are counted first, AYII is counted once, committed next-season credit is counted once, and only the residual verified gap becomes an RLF draw.

Sell surviving output

Record actual gross crop sales — even in severe loss cases.

2
Deduct only explicit obligations

Harvest/marketing, fixed post-harvest, debt actually repaid, tenant/current obligations and protected household cash.

3
Retain net proceeds

The residual sales cash remains available for the next sowing cycle.

4
Add claim and committed funds

Count AYII, other liquid cash and committed next-season credit once.

5
Draw the verified residual

RLF fills only the remaining successor-season cash-COP gap, subject to its credit cap.

“Insurance restores the insured loss. Renewal liquidity preserves the farmer’s ability to plant the next crop. V12.5 keeps the two functions economically and actuarially distinct.”

Policy-maker takeaway

Granular pricing does not require abandoning a national programme.

A national crop anchor can coexist with district-level risk differentiation, transparent support, catastrophe protection and a separate renewal-liquidity facility. V12.5 provides the calculation spine; the next decisions are commercial and institutional.
