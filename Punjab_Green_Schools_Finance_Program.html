Market Failure & Rationale

Problem Being Addressed & Strategic Fit

Low-cost private schools face a structural, not technical, financing constraint — despite daytime load profiles naturally suited to solar generation.

1The Financing Constraint

Balance-Sheet Barriers
  • Limited formal collateral
  • Small ticket sizes
  • Weak or informal financial statements
Cash-Flow Barriers
  • Variable fee collection
  • Dependence on verified enrollment or PEF payments
  • Commercial lending rates absorb too much of the energy saving
Structural / Program Barriers
  • Uncertainty on roof ownership, leases, maintenance
  • Lender origination costs for a dispersed portfolio
  • Uncertainty in stacking public and climate risk-sharing instruments
Core insight A properly structured program can convert electricity expenditure into an asset-finance repayment — while preserving immediate savings for the school throughout the loan tenor.

Material eligibility gap Many standalone low-cost schools likely fall below PKR 30 million in annual turnover — classifying as Micro Enterprises. The 2024 circular states coverage only for Small and Medium Enterprises. The program must not assume SBP coverage for every school until SBP/Finance Division provides written clarification or extends the scheme to Micro Enterprises. The proposed waterfall (Section 5) addresses this by design — Government of Punjab backstops the same 10% coverage rate where SBP does not apply — but this still requires formal GoPunjab budget commitment and documentation before it can be relied upon.

  • Supports scale beyond remaining GCF capacity
  • Coordinates public and private risk-sharing without permitting double recovery
  • Helps crowd in Punjab Government support and technical standards
  • Creates a replicable green-SME model extensible to clinics, vocational institutions, retailers, and other daytime-load SMEs

Product Structure — Core Mechanic

Risk-Sharing Waterfall

Five-layer economic waterfall coordinating borrower protection, SBP, GCF, NCGCL, and JS Bank — engineered against double recovery.

Core principle The waterfall is a fixed, sequential-tranche structure — SBP/GoPunjab (10%), GCF (20%), and NCGCL (20%) each cover a defined, non-overlapping share of net loss, with JS Bank retaining the residual 50%. Layers are additive by design, not independently duplicative: agreements must still ensure aggregate payments never exceed 100% of verified net principal loss, recoveries are shared or returned per each scheme, and JS Bank retains meaningful risk and servicing incentives throughout.

1. Borrower & Transaction Protection
First line of defense, before any guarantor is called

6NCGCL Guarantee Pricing REQUIRES RE-CALIBRATION

Pricing note The indicative 0.75%–1.25% fee range below was calibrated for a tail/gap layer with near-zero expected normal-case payout. Under the new 20% defined-tranche structure, NCGCL's expected loss is materially higher (Section 3 above) and this pricing range should be re-run through NCGCL's expected-loss and capital-allocation model before being offered to JS Bank.
  • 0.75%–1.25% per annum on NCGCL guaranteed outstanding exposure (previous indicative range; requires re-calibration for 20% coverage)
  • Lower pricing for PEF-linked schools with controlled collections and strong performance
  • Higher pricing for independent schools, weaker cash-flow visibility, or shorter roof tenure
  • Punjab may pay the pilot guarantee fee as program support, subject to GCF and public-finance rules
  • Final pricing follows NCGCL's approved guarantee, capital-allocation, expected-loss, and return framework
