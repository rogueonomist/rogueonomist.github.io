Thar Coal Power Cluster — Utilization & Energy Gap Analysis

Four indigenous coal plants (2,640 MW rated / 2,435 MW dependable) across Thar Block-I and Block-II. Full FY 2024–25 data (ISMO) extended through March 2026 (CPPA-G energy purchase invoices). Combined dataset: 17 months of Thar dispatch and fuel charge data.
FY2024-25: Jul 2024 – Jun 2025 · ISMO hourly file (complete) FY2025-26: Aug 2025 – Mar 2026 · CPPA-G Energy Purchase Data V4 (5 months, gaps noted) Plants: TCB-I · Engro Thar · ThalNova · TEL Prepared by: Ammar H. Khan
Data coverage note: FY2025-26 data from CPPA-G Energy Purchase V4 covers Aug, Sep, Dec 2025 and Jan, Mar 2026. Missing months: Jul 2025 (no Thar invoices), Nov 2025, Feb 2026. Oct 2025 entry is identical to Sep 2025 (likely preliminary placeholder — excluded). TEL did not submit an invoice for Jan 2026 (treated as zero/offline). Mar 2026 TCB-I, ThalNova, and TEL figures match Dec 2025 values exactly; Engro Mar 2026 figure (378 GWh) is unique and verified.
Extended-period finding: Across 17 months of available data (Jul 2024 – Mar 2026), the Thar cluster generated 22,193 GWh against a dependable-capacity ceiling of 30,330 GWh — a cumulative energy gap of 8,137 GWh at a system-level average CUF of 73.2%. FY2025-26 shows slight improvement (73.8% avg CUF vs 72.9% in FY2024-25) but TEL's Jan 2026 outage and Engro/ThalNova's Sep 2025 collapse (47% / 50% CUF) confirm the cluster's recurring single-plant shock vulnerability persists into the new fiscal year.

FY2025-26 Early Signal
The FY2025-26 partial data (5 months) shows TCB-I improving to 81.5% CUF (vs 77.1% in FY2024-25) — the anchor plant is performing better. However, the smaller plants continue to drag: Engro Thar dropped to 68.7% (from 73.1%), ThalNova to 64.9% (from 67.4%), and TEL was offline entirely in January 2026. The September 2025 event — where Engro collapsed to 47% CUF and ThalNova to 50% simultaneously — mirrors the cluster-wide stress of Q2 FY2024-25 and points to a recurring September inflection in coal supply logistics or post-monsoon plant cycling.

Unutilized Capacity — Jul 2024 – Mar 2026
8,137 GWh not generated across 17 months of available data
Across the 17 months with Thar generation data (12 complete FY2024-25 months + 5 FY2025-26 months), the cluster's cumulative energy gap reaches 8,137 GWh — energy that was fully funded through unconditional capacity payments but never delivered to the grid. The gap in FY2025-26 is running at a pace of ~471 GWh/month (2,354 GWh ÷ 5 months), compared to 482 GWh/month in FY2024-25 — a marginal improvement driven primarily by TCB-I's stronger performance, partially offset by continued volatility at Engro, ThalNova, and TEL.

Trajectory Assessment
TCB-I is the single plant showing genuine improvement (+4.4 pp CUF year-over-year for comparable months), suggesting operational maturation of the largest unit. Engro, ThalNova, and TEL are stagnant or declining — Engro's September 2025 dip to 47% is structurally concerning and mirrors its September 2024 collapse (55.7%), pointing to a recurring monsoon-season logistics or mine output constraint. TEL's chronic underperformance (60–62% across both FYs) and complete January 2026 absence indicate an unresolved plant-level issue. Every percentage point gained on the 2,435 MW dependable base delivers ~214 GWh/year of additional indigenous output.
Severe Underperformance Events — Jul 2024 – Mar 2026
Plant-months with CUF below 50% — the recurring shock pattern
The extended dataset confirms that severe underperformance events (CUF <50%) are not confined to FY2024-25. September 2025 brought simultaneous Engro (47.1%) and ThalNova (49.6%) collapses, and TEL submitted no generation invoice for January 2026 — its fourth distinct near-outage episode across 17 months. TCB-I has had only one severe event (November 2024) in 17 months; the smaller Block-II units are structurally more volatile.

Structural Diagnosis — 17-Month Evidence
Three clear failure modes emerge from the extended dataset: (1) TEL chronic fragility — 4 outage events in 17 months (Sep 2024, Dec 2024, Feb 2025, Jan 2026); this plant has not achieved a sustained run above 80% CUF in any two consecutive months across the dataset. (2) September syndrome — Engro and ThalNova both collapse in September (both fiscal years), consistent with post-monsoon mine access issues at SECMC Block-II or coal stockpile drawdown during peak monsoon coal transport disruption. (3) TCB-I November 2024 anomaly — the 34% CUF event was singular and not repeated; the plant recovered to 79%+ from January 2025 onward, suggesting a resolved forced outage rather than a systemic problem. Root cause investigation priority: TEL > September logistics > Engro/ThalNova Block-II coal supply chain.
Fuel Cost Data — CPPA-G Energy Purchase Invoices
Variable energy cost per plant — available for FY2025-26 months from CPPA-G V4
The CPPA-G Energy Purchase Data V4 file introduces a new dimension not available in the ISMO dataset: fuel charges (PKR) per plant per month. This is the variable energy purchase cost (EPP component) reflecting coal consumption, heat rates, and applicable adjustments. These figures are distinct from the unconditional Capacity Purchase Price — they are paid only when generation actually occurs. Below the per-kWh fuel cost can be inferred.

Cost Efficiency Signal
The fuel charge data reveals that months with lower generation (Sep 2025 for Engro at 47% CUF) show elevated per-kWh costs — not because coal is more expensive, but because fixed fuel-linked adjustments (heat degradation, load correction factors) are spread over fewer units. This creates a perverse cost dynamic: the less these plants run, the more expensive each MWh becomes, eroding the economic merit-order advantage that justifies their priority dispatch position. Full utilisation at 90%+ CUF would reduce effective fuel cost per kWh by an estimated 15–20% for Engro and ThalNova.

Policy Priority
The Thar cluster's effective cost of PKR 30.1/kWh is entirely a utilization penalty — if fully deployed at 90% CUF, cost falls to ~PKR 24.3/kWh, making it cheaper than RLNG on an all-in basis. The economic case for operational reform is not incremental: a 17 percentage point CUF improvement (from 73% to 90%) across 2,435 MW would save approximately PKR 35 billion annually in RLNG displacement costs while eliminating the per-unit capacity cost penalty. The financial incentive exists; the constraint is operational — mine logistics, plant reliability, and transmission scheduling at the Thar-grid interface.
