Section A

Executive Snapshot

The refinery sector’s gross margin cycle peaked in FY22, compressed into a five-year low in FY25, and then rebounded sharply in 1QFY26/Q3FY26 as product cracks and ex-refinery economics improved. The key insight is that refinery earnings are driven by margin, not revenue.

Section D

GRM → Gross Profit → PAT: The Pass-Through Mechanics

The sector behaves like an operating-leverage machine. Small movements in gross margin can create large changes in PAT because salaries, utilities, finance cost, depreciation, tax and working-capital burdens do not fall proportionately when cracks compress.

Why revenue can mislead

PRL: almost same 9M revenue, opposite earnings outcome

PRL’s 9MFY25 and 9MFY26 net revenue were broadly similar, but gross profit moved from negligible to more than PKR 25bn as margins recovered.

Cnergyico: operating leverage is the story

Higher revenue helped, but the real turnaround came because cost of sales grew far more slowly than revenue, allowing gross profit to surge.

NRL: high beta means high volatility

NRL moved from large losses to profit as gross margin returned, but it remains exposed to inventory timing, crude mix and finance costs.

Section E

What Actually Drives GRM in Pakistan?

A refinery’s margin is determined by product cracks, yield structure, crude cost, logistics, inventory timing and policy treatment. Pakistani refineries remain constrained by low-complexity configurations and furnace-oil drag.

Section F

Implications for Senior Policymakers

The GRM evidence supports a sequenced policy playbook: protect refinery viability, but do not embed inefficient import-cost assumptions in local barrels; accelerate upgrades; and use storage/procurement reform to reduce price spikes.

1. Price Formula
Cost attribution
  • Separate local-refined and imported barrels.
  • Remove non-incurred import incidentals from local product.
  • Keep refineries viable; avoid forced below-cost pricing.
2. Refinery Upgrade
Yield transformation
  • More MS/HSD, less FO.
  • Release deemed-duty support against milestones.
  • Ring-fence escrowed upgrade funds.
3. Procurement
Premium discipline
  • Term supply, premium caps and deferred payment.
  • Monitor product cracks daily.
  • Use crude/product optionality.
4. Storage & PDL
Shock absorption
  • Bonded and sovereign product stocks.
  • Countercyclical PDL.
  • Do not use PDL as lazy tax substitute.
Exhibit F-1
Bottom-line explanation
The sector’s story is simple: Pakistani refineries earn when regional product cracks are high and when their product slate lets them capture those cracks. They suffer when FO drag rises, crude/product prices fall through inventory, or product premiums narrow. The pump-price story is different: GRM affects ex-refinery economics, but the consumer price can still be dominated by import parity assumptions, IFEM, PDL, taxes and margins.

Data Annex and Methodology

Annex A
Definitions used in the dashboard
True GRM = Σ(Product price × Product yield) − Landed crude cost − Freight / procurement cost

Accounting gross margin = Gross profit / Net revenue

True GRM is a trading/economic measure; accounting gross margin is a public-financial-statement proxy. The dashboard separates these explicitly.
