Protect lifeline users
Consumers using 0–100 units should see no change. The reform should not be sold as a shock to low-income households.
2
Make energy prices honest
Use low energy rates when the system is cheap, and higher rates when the system is stressed. The signal should be visible on the bill.
3
Charge for peak fairly
High-use households should pay for the peak capacity they require — but solar and battery users should be rewarded when they actually reduce that peak.
Plain-English reform logic: The current tariff says “every unit is expensive.” A Two-Part Tariff says “the grid is expensive at peak, but energy can be cheap when the system has surplus.” That is a better signal for households, factories, rooftop solar, batteries, and the grid itself.

The communication problem

A technically correct tariff can fail if it is introduced in a way that households experience as punishment. The dashboard therefore separates the message into three groups: lifeline consumers are protected; middle consumers get a softer transitional structure; high-use and prosumer consumers move toward cost-reflective pricing, but only with floors that reward real peak reduction.

The hard red line: a 10 kW net-metering household should not jump from around Rs 4,000 to a fixed charge of Rs 50,425 overnight. That is not a reform path; it is a prosumer backlash path.

Policy ask in plain English
Phase the reform so consumers see a fair path, not a penalty.
PhaseWhat happensWhy it is acceptable
Phase 1
July 2026		All prosumers pay flat Rs 400/kW sanctioned load per month. A 10 kW connection pays Rs 4,000/month. Export credit moves to Rs 7.50/kWh.Simple, implementable without full AMI, and gives DISCOs immediate partial network recovery without extreme bill shock.
Phase 2
AMI rollout		Move to MDI-based capacity charges with floors: 50% grid-only, 35% solar-only, 20% solar+BESS, 40% BESS-only.Consumers who reduce evening peak receive visible savings. This is the core behavioural incentive.
Phase 3
Scale-up		Certify smart inverters and home energy management systems that can verify MDI caps and BESS dispatch.Turns households into measurable demand-response assets and reduces future capacity requirements.

Implementation roadmap
A feasible sequence: protect first, price gradually, then unlock peak demand response.
Phase 0 — now
Approve the design principle
Acknowledge that residential TPT cannot be copied from industrial design without modification. Confirm lifeline protection, transitional HCC and solar/BESS floors.
Phase 1 — July 2026
Flat prosumer charge + protected residential tiers
Introduce Rs 400/kW-SL/month for prosumers, retain protection for 0–100 units, and use a no-worse-off floor for middle tiers during transition.
Phase 2 — FY2027
AMI-based MDI billing
Mandate AMI for high-use households, net-metering consumers and registered BESS owners. Activate 35% and 20% floors.
Phase 3 — FY2028+
Verified home energy management
Certify inverters and battery systems that can cap MDI. Treat households as demand-response assets rather than only consumers.
